## How it's worked out

Prices are the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, the government's main measure of what households pay, from the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/). The change in prices between two dates is the index at the later date divided by the index at the earlier one. A whole year uses the average of that year's monthly readings.

"To match prices" is the earlier amount multiplied by the later index and divided by the earlier one. "Buys less" or "buys more" compares the change in your amount with the change in prices: the later amount divided by the earlier one, then divided by the later index over the earlier index, minus one.

The index is a national average across hundreds of goods and services, weighted by what urban households spend. [What Inflation Is](what-is-inflation.html) explains what it covers and leaves out, and [Real vs. Nominal](real-vs-nominal.html) explains why a raise can buy less.
