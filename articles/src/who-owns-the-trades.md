## The short version

There are about 124,000 plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractors in the United States and about 100,000 electrical contractors, and their numbers are still growing. Most are small. Since the mid-2010s, private equity firms have been buying some of the larger local ones and combining them into national groups with dozens of brands; private equity deals for heating and cooling companies in 2024 were the most in at least seven years. The buyers usually keep the local name, the trucks and often the founder. Owners sell because the price is good, because many are near retirement, and because a national group can pay more for a business than another local shop can. What changes for customers is mostly unmeasured: we found no study comparing prices or quality under the new owners, only reporting that cuts both ways. Most of these purchases are too small to require notice to federal antitrust regulators, which is why the government tracks them poorly. Nobody publishes what share of the trade the national groups now own.

## The analogy

A hotel on the town square that has kept its old name, its old sign and its old staff, but now books through a national chain's system, buys its towels through the chain's contracts and answers to the chain's managers. A guest who walks in sees the same hotel. What changed is who owns it, who sets its prices and where its profit goes.

The hotel is the local plumbing or air-conditioning company. The chain is the national group, and behind it, the private equity fund that owns the group.

Figures below are as of early November 2026.

## How big the trade is

The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 123,866 plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractor establishments in 2025, up from 103,126 in 2016, and 100,285 electrical contractors, up from 83,200 ([BLS, plumbing and HVAC](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/ENUUS00020523822); [BLS, electrical](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/ENUUS00020523821)). These are counts of locations with employees; plumbers and technicians who work alone aren't in them. In 2023, 64,871 of the 111,207 plumbing and HVAC contractor locations with employees, more than half, had fewer than five ([Census Bureau, County Business Patterns](https://data.census.gov/table/CBP2023.CB2300CBP?codeset=naics%7E23822&g=010XX00US)). Together the two trades employed about 2.5 million people in August 2026 ([BLS, plumbing and HVAC](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CES2023822001); [BLS, electrical](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CES2023821001)).

**What isn't known.** We found no public source that counts how many of these companies are owned by private equity or by national groups. PitchBook, which tracks private deals, doesn't publish an estimate in its analysis of the trade, and the market-research figures that circulate online don't show their methods. The share is unknown, and probably still small by count, since the buyers target the larger companies.

## How the buying works

Private equity firms raise money from investors such as pension funds, university endowments, insurers and wealthy individuals, buy companies, try to make them more profitable and sell them, often within several years ([SEC, Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/private-investment-funds/private-equity)). In the trades, as with vet clinics and dental offices, the method is called a roll-up. A fund buys one sizable regional company, the platform, then uses it to buy smaller local ones, the add-ons, and runs them on shared systems: dispatch software, call centers, purchasing, recruiting, training and marketing.

Part of the profit comes from how the companies are priced. A small, owner-run shop typically sells for about three to eight times its yearly earnings, while a large group with steady, recurring revenue can be valued in the mid-teens ([Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/publications/ma-residential-hvac-services-industry)). Buy enough shops at the lower price and the combined company can be worth more than the parts cost. The recurring revenue comes largely from maintenance memberships, a yearly or monthly fee for tune-ups and priority service; Champions Group, one of the larger groups, reports about 150,000 members ([Orange County Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/oc-homepage/blackstone-to-acquire-irvines-champions-group/)).

PitchBook counted 55 private equity deals in its heating, ventilation and air-conditioning category, which includes equipment makers, in North America and Europe in 2024: up 72% from 2023 and the most since at least 2017 ([PitchBook](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/Q2_2025_PitchBook_Analyst_Note_Clearing_the_Air_on_HVAC_19463.pdf)). Of 25 HVAC deals that PKF Investment Banking, an affiliate of the accounting firm PKF O'Connor Davies, highlighted for the first half of 2026, across manufacturing, distribution and mostly commercial service companies, 17 had buyers backed by private equity, 14 of them add-ons ([PKF O'Connor Davies](https://www.pkfod.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/US-HVAC-MA-Industry-Update-Summer-2026.pdf)).

## Who the big buyers are

A handful of groups lead, most of them owned by private equity firms, and several have themselves been sold from one fund to another:

- **Apex Service Partners**, backed by Alpine Investors: 75 local brands in 46 states and more than $3 billion in revenue. In May 2026 Apollo agreed to buy a minority stake ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.com/insights-news/pressreleases/2026/05/apex-service-partners-and-alpine-investors-announce-strategic-mi)); Reuters, citing an unnamed source, reported a valuation of about $10 billion including debt ([Reuters, via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/05/27/apex-service-partners-nears-minority-stake-sale-at-10-billion-valuation-source-says/)).
- **Wrench Group**, owned by Leonard Green, TSG Consumer Partners and Oak Hill Capital ([Leonard Green](https://www.leonardgreen.com/tsg-consumer-partners-oak-hill-partner-with-leonard-green-and-management-to-enhance-wrench-groups-next-phase-of-growth/)): 24 brands in 26 markets and more than 7,400 employees ([Wrench Group](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/our-story)).
- **Champions Group**, built by Odyssey Investment Partners from Service Champions in Southern California: more than 20 local brands and 2,400 employees ([Orange County Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/oc-homepage/blackstone-to-acquire-irvines-champions-group/)). Blackstone agreed in February 2026 to buy it, with Odyssey and management keeping a significant minority stake ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/blackstone-announces-agreement-to-acquire-champions-group/)); HarbourVest, an investor in the company, reported the sale completed that month, with existing shareholders keeping 30% ([HarbourVest, March 2026 report](https://harbourvest.com/content/dam/hv/web/files/en/funds/hpif/HPIF%20Report%20-%20March%202026.pdf)). The price wasn't disclosed; Bloomberg, citing unnamed sources, put it at about $2.5 billion ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/blackstone-is-said-to-near-2-5-billion-deal-for-champions-group)).
- **Sila Services** had more than 30 brands, mostly in the East and Midwest, when Goldman Sachs Alternatives agreed in November 2024 to buy a majority stake from Morgan Stanley Capital Partners ([Goldman Sachs](https://am.gs.com/en-us/institutions/news/press-release/2024/sila-services-investment)).
- **Service Experts**, with more than 75 locations in 31 states ([Service Experts](https://www.serviceexperts.com/about-us/)), is owned by Brookfield Infrastructure, which bought its former parent, Enercare, in 2018 ([Brookfield](https://bip.brookfield.com/press-releases/bip/brookfield-infrastructure-completes-c43-billion-acquisition-enercare-inc)).
- **Neighborly**, owned by KKR since 2021, works differently: it franchises brands such as Mr. Rooter and Mr. Electric to local owners rather than buying the companies outright ([KKR, via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210708005304/en/KKR-to-Acquire-Leading-Home-Services-Platform-Neighborly%C2%AE); [Mr. Rooter](https://www.mrrooter.com/about/mr-rooter-brand-entity/)).

The same thing is happening in commercial heating and cooling, where Bain Capital and Abu Dhabi's sovereign fund Mubadala bought Service Logic in December 2025 ([Mubadala](https://www.mubadala.com/en/news/mubadala-partners-with-bain-capital-in-acquisition-of-service-logic)), but this page is about the companies that come to houses.

## The name on the truck

Most groups keep the name of the company they buy. When Champions Group bought Lex Cooling, Heating & Plumbing in North Texas in January 2026, Powell Electric in Southern California in August 2026 and Bardi Heating, Cooling, Plumbing and Electric near Atlanta in November 2026, each kept its name ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/165723-champions-group-acquires-lex-cooling-heating-plumbing-and-electrical); [Champions Group, via Napa Valley Register](https://napavalleyregister.com/online_features/press_releases/champions-group-expands-southern-california-footprint-with-acquisition-of-powell-electric/article_d2834896-1e70-519e-916a-b31fa08aea1e.html); [Champions Group, via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/champions-group-marks-first-acquisition-in-the-southeast-with-addition-of-bardi-heating-cooling-plumbing-and-electric-302926870.html)).

The groups say the local name is the asset they're buying. Wrench Group says each of its brands has earned its community's trust and that its role is "to strengthen what they've built — not change it" ([Wrench Group](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/our-story)). Not every group keeps the name: in 2025 Service Experts renamed four companies it had bought in the Dallas area under its own brand ([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/dallas-fort-worth/richardson/business/2025/06/10/four-hvac-and-plumbing-companies-rebranding-under-richardson-based-service-experts)).

## Why owners sell

**Age.** In 2018, 51% of the owners of American businesses with employees were 55 or older ([Census Bureau, Annual Business Survey](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/visualizations/2020/comm/business-owners-ages.pdf)). That figure covers all industries; advisers on these deals describe founders who have run their companies for decades as a main source of sellers ([Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/publications/ma-residential-hvac-services-industry)).

**Price.** A national group can pay more than a local competitor, for the reasons above.

**People.** Skilled technicians are scarce. The BLS projects jobs for heating and air-conditioning mechanics to grow 11% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average, with about 40,600 openings a year ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm)). Larger companies can offer better deals on benefits such as health care, and sometimes better pay ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2024/10/24/private-equity-buying-up-businesses-in-the-skilled-trades-hvac-plumbing-electrician)), which helps them recruit.

**Demand.** Investors describe the work as essential and recurring, with little equipment to buy ([Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/publications/ma-residential-hvac-services-industry)). Federal tax credits for heat pumps and other efficiency upgrades, which had pushed demand up, ended for equipment installed after December 31, 2025, under the 2025 tax law ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/164868-big-beautiful-bill-has-positives-and-negatives-for-hvac-industry)).

## What changes for customers

Less is known than either side claims. We found no study comparing prices, sales practices or the quality of work at private equity–owned home-service companies with independent ones. The research on private equity ownership has focused on health care, where a 2023 review of 55 studies found it most consistently associated with higher costs to patients or payers and mixed-to-harmful effects on quality ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/382/bmj-2023-075244)); whether that carries over to a trade with many competitors and no insurers in the middle hasn't been studied.

What exists is reporting, and it points both ways. One former technician at a company bought by a private equity–owned group told The American Prospect that the focus shifted to replacing equipment and estimated that warranty calls doubled ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/environment/2023-10-16-private-equity-intensifies-rollups-hvac/)). A Wall Street Journal reporter who covered the trend described the results as mixed: some customers say technicians know less, others that they now get faster response times ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2024/10/24/private-equity-buying-up-businesses-in-the-skilled-trades-hvac-plumbing-electrician)). Membership plans predate private equity ownership, too: Service Champions sold them for years before Odyssey bought it ([Orange County Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/oc-homepage/blackstone-to-acquire-irvines-champions-group/)), so a plan alone doesn't show who owns a company.

## What changes for workers

The evidence here is also anecdotal: reports of better benefits and sometimes better pay at larger groups ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2024/10/24/private-equity-buying-up-businesses-in-the-skilled-trades-hvac-plumbing-electrician)), and another of technicians leaving after an acquisition ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/environment/2023-10-16-private-equity-intensifies-rollups-hvac/)).

Two rules matter. The Federal Trade Commission's 2024 rule banning most noncompete agreements was struck down by a federal court in Texas in August 2024 ([Ryan LLC v. FTC](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txnd.389064/gov.uscourts.txnd.389064.212.0_1.pdf)), and in September 2025 the FTC dropped its appeal, leaving noncompetes to state law and to case-by-case enforcement ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule)). California, whose law has long voided most noncompetes ([Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=16600)), has since January 1, 2026 also barred most agreements that make workers repay training costs if they leave, with exceptions such as state-approved apprenticeships ([Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/10/29/california-enacts-ab-692-a-new-ban-on-stay-or-pay-employment-contracts)).

## Why regulators rarely see these deals

Companies must notify the FTC and the Justice Department before a merger only if the deal is worth more than a threshold, $133.9 million for deals closing on or after February 17, 2026 ([Dechert](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/1/minimum-hsr-reporting-threshold-rises-to-us-133-9-million--inter.html)). Local shops sell for a small fraction of that, so most add-ons are never reported, and regulators can't count or review what they don't see. The large platform sales are reported, but each one, viewed alone, usually combines companies that didn't compete much with each other.

In May 2024 the FTC and the Justice Department asked the public for information on "serial acquisitions," naming construction and repair among the sectors of interest ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/05/ftc-doj-seek-info-serial-acquisitions-roll-strategies-across-us-economy)). The agencies haven't published findings from it. The current FTC has kept the issue alive in another industry: in a proposed settlement in May 2026 over self-checkout kiosks for workplace "micromarkets," it would require 365 Retail Markets to give notice before acquiring any business in that market, and Commissioner Mark Meador pointed to the company's serial acquisitions, including some below the reporting threshold ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-07/html/2026-09021.htm)). We found no such case involving home services. New state laws in Washington and Colorado, and one taking effect in California in 2027, require copies of federal merger filings, so they reach only deals that already meet the federal threshold ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/08/mini-hsr-acts-take-effect-in-washington-and-colorado-with-similar-legislation-pending-in-other-states); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/california-adopts-uniform-antitrust-premerger-notification-regime)).

## When roll-ups go wrong

The strategy has failed before. Encompass Services, formed from 1990s roll-ups of heating, plumbing and electrical contractors by publicly traded companies, had $3.9 billion in revenue and $815 million in debt in 2001 and filed for bankruptcy in November 2002 ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/90083-encompass-submits-plan-to-restructure-under-chapter-11)). More recently Air Pros USA, which had bought 11 brands, filed for bankruptcy in March 2025 with more than $250 million in debt; its lender had removed the founder as manager the year before, and it agreed to sell its businesses in six separate deals ([HomePros News](https://homepros.news/inside-air-pros-bankruptcy-filing/)). Private equity–backed groups typically borrow heavily; Wrench Group, for example, refinanced with $1.3 billion of private debt in 2025 ([Secured Finance Network](https://www.sfnet.com/home/industry-data-publications/the-secured-lender/tsl-express-daily-articles-news/tsl-express-daily-articles-news/2025/09/15/blue-owl-oak-hill-lead-$1.3-billion-debt-refinancing-for-wrench-group)). A group that overpays or fails to combine its companies can fail even when its trucks are busy.

## Finding out who owns a company

The groups generally list their brands on their own websites, and acquisitions are usually announced in press releases. California requires contractors to show their business name and license number on work vehicles and their license number in ads, but not the name of a parent company ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2020/20-21_Advertising_on_Vehicles.pdf)). The Contractors State License Board's online lookup shows who holds a company's license and who its officers are ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/onlineservices/checklicenseII/checklicense.aspx)).

## Where the analogy breaks

A hotel guest can usually see the chain's name on the booking. A homeowner calling for a repair often can't, and calls rarely, sometimes in an emergency, with little chance to compare. And a hotel chain mostly runs hotels; a private equity fund owns a company for some years and then sells it, so the owner a homeowner has today may not be the owner in five years.

## What people argue about

**The local name.** The groups say keeping a name preserves what customers trust, now with more resources behind it ([Wrench Group](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/our-story)). The commentator Matt Walsh argued in October 2026 that keeping old names lets homeowners think they are comparing competitors when the companies share an owner; ACHR News's editorial director wrote in response that homeowners who learn this "will feel misled" even if nobody technically misrepresented anything ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/blogs/17-opinions/post/166923-political-commentator-bashes-hvac-private-equity)).

**Prices and service.** Supporters say scale brings capital, technology, training, benefits and marketing that small shops can't afford; ACHR News's editorial director listed those benefits in the same column. Walsh, as the column summarized him, argued that private equity consolidation is contributing to higher prices and more aggressive sales tactics ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/blogs/17-opinions/post/166923-political-commentator-bashes-hvac-private-equity)). Neither side has published data on home services specifically.

**Owners and workers.** For a retiring owner, a national group can make an offer no local buyer can match, and for a technician a large employer can mean better benefits ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2024/10/24/private-equity-buying-up-businesses-in-the-skilled-trades-hvac-plumbing-electrician)); the ACHR column counts succession among the benefits. Reporting from inside acquired companies has also found technicians leaving after an acquisition ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/environment/2023-10-16-private-equity-intensifies-rollups-hvac/)).

**Whether regulators should look.** Former FTC Chair Lina Khan said in 2024 that firms can use serial acquisitions to roll up markets and undermine competition ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/05/ftc-doj-seek-info-serial-acquisitions-roll-strategies-across-us-economy)). The U.S. Chamber of Commerce has argued that more merger reporting burdens the great majority of deals, which raise no competitive concern, and that private equity deals aren't inherently anticompetitive ([U.S. Chamber of Commerce](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/20230927-U.S.-Chamber-HSR-Comments-and-Kothari-Report.pdf)).
