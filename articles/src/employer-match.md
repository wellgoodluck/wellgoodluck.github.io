## How it's worked out

Your employer's match is its rate times what you put in, counting only what you put in up to the formula's limit. With a second step, the second rate applies to what you put in on the next slice of pay. The full match is what you'd get by putting in the whole of both slices. The match left behind is the full match less the match you get.

What it could grow to is in future dollars: prices will rise over the years too, so the sum would buy less than it does today. It assumes the amount left behind is invested at the start of each year, from your age now through the year before you retire, at the same return every year, with no taxes or fees. Real returns go up and down, and nobody can promise any rate. Pay and contributions usually rise over a career, which this leaves out.

Matching money may not be yours right away. Under federal law, if the match vests all at once, a plan can make you wait up to three years; if it vests a bit each year, up to six.

Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere. A link carries your numbers only if you copy one with them.
