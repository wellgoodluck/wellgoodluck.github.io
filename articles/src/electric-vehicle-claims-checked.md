## In one sentence

Most of the alarming claims about electric cars were true of first-generation cars, are true at interstate speed or in deep cold, or hold in one region rather than nationally; most of the glowing ones are true only for owners who charge at home; and the two that hold up everywhere are that EV fires are rarer but harder to put out, and that the extra weight is a real concern for everyone else on the road.

## The claims

Fifteen claims heard in both directions, for and against, each checked against the best current data. Where a claim has a real basis, that is stated too. The costs, the charging figures and the studies behind these verdicts are laid out in part one, [The Case For and the Case Against](electric-vehicles-case-for-and-against.html). Figures are as of late October 2026.

**"You'll get stranded. Range anxiety."** The newest sub-$40,000 EVs are rated at 255–321 miles; 86% of charging is done at home; the U.S. has more than 80,000 public fast-charging ports and 12% of charging attempts fail, down from 19% in 2024. Rural counties lag: 45% had any fast charger in early 2025 versus 77% of metro counties ([J.D. Power](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/news/55398458/public-ev-charging-reliability-improves-as-newer-fast-charging-networks-raise-the-bar); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/ev-charging/map-rural-fast-chargers-trump)). *Verdict:* mostly outdated for daily driving with home charging; still real for rural road trips and renters.

**"The battery dies after a few years and costs $20,000."** Modern packs lose about 1.8% of capacity a year, and Recurrent data reported by the Wall Street Journal put range retention at up to 95% after five years. 0.3% of EVs built since 2022 have needed a replacement, versus about 1 in 12 built 2011–2016 (the early uncooled Nissan Leaf). Packs carry at least 8-year, 100,000-mile warranties; out-of-warranty replacement runs $5,000–$17,000 ([Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/uk/press-release/2024-battery-degradation); [Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/research/how-long-do-ev-batteries-last); [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/articles/how-much-is-an-electric-car-battery)). *Verdict:* not supported for current EVs; it was true of first-generation cars.

**"EVs catch fire more than gas cars."** Swedish, Norwegian and Tesla fleet data all show the opposite (Poland's 2025 figures are the outlier, at parity): Sweden 0.45 fires per 10,000 EVs against 7.5 for other cars; Norway 0.55 against 3.9; Tesla's fleet one fire per 140 million miles against one per 17 million for U.S. vehicles overall. But EV battery fires are harder to put out (3,000–8,000 gallons of water), can reignite, and saltwater flooding is a real trigger: 36 of the 3,000–5,000 EVs flooded by Hurricane Ian burned. In New York City, most lithium-battery fire deaths involve e-bikes and scooters, not cars ([Cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1074122); [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/767908/ev-fire-safety-data-2025/); [Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/VehicleFireSafetyReport); [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/weather/2024-10-11/after-hurricane-milton-growing-risk-flooded-electric-cars-going-up-flames); [FDNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/fdny/news/03-25/fdny-commissioner-robert-s-tucker-significant-progress-the-battle-against-lithium-ion-battery-fires)). *Verdict:* not supported on how often; true on how hard to extinguish.

**"EVs are dirtier than gas cars once you count the battery and coal power."** Building an EV emits 30–100% more than building a gas car; the debt is repaid in 13,500–21,300 miles on the U.S. grid and lifetime emissions end 40–60% lower. On 2024 grid data the average EV equals a 100-mpg gas car nationally; even the lowest-scoring grid region UCS reports (Michigan's, at 62 mpg-equivalent) beats the average new gas car. The claim was closer to true in 2013, when coal supplied roughly double its current share of about 16% of U.S. power ([Argonne, via Reuters](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/reu20210629KCN2E50AP/); [MIT 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/us-drivers-electric-vehicles-offer-emissions-benefits-and-cost-savings-0512); [UCS](https://blog.ucs.org/dave-reichmuth/epa-stopped-updating-the-data-behind-electric-vehicle-emissions-the-numbers-still-show-evs-are-winners/); [EIA](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/10/07/292552.htm)). *Verdict:* not supported nationally; partly true in a few coal-heavy regions, where a 57-mpg hybrid comes close.

**"The grid can't handle everyone charging."** EV charging used about 0.2% of U.S. electricity in 2023; data centers used 4.4% and are forecast at 7–12% by 2028. Full electrification of everything would raise 2050 demand 20–38%, at growth rates below the 1950–2016 average. In a California utility pilot, 85% of home chargers were already set to charge off-peak. The real constraint is local: a Level 2 charger is the biggest load in a house, and a street of them starting at 6 pm can overload a neighborhood transformer without staggered or time-of-use charging ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=62083); [LBNL](https://energyanalysis.lbl.gov/news/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers); [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy18osti/72096.pdf); [Peninsula Clean Energy](https://library.peninsulacleanenergy.com/m/91b5939732755c7/original/EV-Managed-Charging-Pilot-Final-Report.pdf)). *Verdict:* not supported at the national level; real at the neighborhood-transformer level.

**"California told people not to charge their EVs, proof the grid is failing."** The September 1, 2022 Flex Alert asked residents to shift all discretionary use (dishwashers, dryers, EVs) out of the 4–9 pm heat-wave peak. No outages occurred, and EV charging is under 1% of California's load even at peak ([NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/california-flex-alert-dont-charge-ev/2992997/); [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/greenvehicles/electric-vehicle-myths)). *Verdict:* misleading.

**"EVs don't work in the cold."** At or below 32°F, EVs lose about 20% of range on average (17% with a heat pump, 25% without), and fast charging slows unless the battery is preconditioned. The January 2024 Chicago incident involved near-empty cars that had not preconditioned, not failed chargers. Norway, where 96% of new cars sold in 2025 were electric, is the standing counterexample ([Recurrent, via CarScoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/01/evs-lose-20-range-on-average-in-freezing-conditions-study-finds/); [Consumer Guide](https://blog.consumerguide.com/makes/tesla/); [Reuters, via GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/motoring/971455/norway-s-new-car-sales-were-96-electric-in-2025/story/)). *Verdict:* partly true, a real penalty rather than a dealbreaker.

**"Hot climates ruin the battery."** Range drops about 5% at 90°F and 15% at 100°F, mostly from air conditioning. Geotab's data tie faster aging to heat combined with frequent fast charging, not to heat alone, and the fleet-wide gap between ideal and average conditions is about 0.2 points a year (1.6% against 1.8%); parking in shade and a lower daily charge limit are what the sources name as offsetting it ([Recurrent, via InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/763596/evs-best-summer-driving-range/); [Geotab, via Green Car Reports](https://www.greencarreports.com/news/1144490_study-never-mind-degradation-batteries-will-outlast-evs-in-most-cases)). *Verdict:* partly true, a small effect with modern thermal management.

**"EPA range numbers are fiction."** At a steady 75 mph, EVs average 12.5% below their label while gas cars beat theirs by 4%. In Edmunds' mixed real-world loop, most current EVs meet or beat their EPA rating, with GM, Hyundai and Kia, Mercedes and Porsche typically over and Rivian and Lucid under. Towing cuts range roughly in half, as it does for gas trucks ([SAE and Car and Driver, via Autoblog](https://autos.yahoo.com/sae-paper-says-evs-worse-130000315.html); [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/electric-car-range-and-consumption-epa-vs-edmunds.html); [Car and Driver](https://autos.yahoo.com/tested-ev-pickups-trailer-towing-130000822.html)). *Verdict:* partly true at interstate speed; not around town.

**"Batteries can't be recycled and end up in landfills."** The "only 5% recycled" figure is a 2016 collection statistic misread as a recycling rate; a 2023 estimate puts global lithium-ion recycling at about 59%, and most jurisdictions ban landfilling EV packs. Packs are worth thousands of dollars in metals; 73% of retired EV packs in 2023 went to second-life use first. The U.S. recycling industry's problem is too few dead batteries, not too many: Li-Cycle went bankrupt and Ascend Elements filed for Chapter 11, squeezed by scarce feedstock and falling metal prices ([Hannah Ritchie](https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/battery-recycling-myth); [Circular Energy Storage, via Auto Recycling World](https://autorecyclingworld.com/global-battery-recycling-volumes-to-rise-sharply-after-2030-new-data-from-ces/); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/recycling-renewables/li-cycles-quest-to-recycle-lithium-ion-batteries-ends-in-bankruptcy)). *Verdict:* not supported.

**"Battery mining is worse than drilling for oil."** An EV needs about six times the mineral inputs of a gas car, and cobalt sourcing is concentrated in the DRC. Over a car's life, though, roughly 30 kg of battery metals are lost after recycling versus about 17,000 liters of gasoline burned; the world extracts and burns about 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuels a year, roughly 500 times the critical-mineral tonnage needed ([IEA, via S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/natural-gas/050621-iea-calls-for-government-action-to-ensure-supply-of-critical-battery-minerals); [Transport & Environment](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/batteries-vs-oil-comparison-raw-material-needs); [Carbon Brief](https://interactive.carbonbrief.org/factcheck/electric-vehicles/)). *Verdict:* partly true; the mining burden is real, front-loaded and mostly recoverable.

**"EVs are toys for the wealthy."** The average new EV sells for $55,879 versus $50,562 for all vehicles, an 11% gap down from roughly 39% in 2022 by CarEdge's tally of Kelley Blue Book data. New EVs now start under $30,000 (Chevrolet Bolt) and $37,000 (Tesla Model 3); the average used EV lists at $34,821, $1,334 above the average used gas car, and 18 of 26 brands' used EVs are cheaper than their gas equivalents. The real divide is housing: homeowners with a driveway capture the savings, renters mostly do not ([Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/sept-2026-atp-report/); [CarEdge](https://caredge.com/guides/average-price-of-an-electric-car-2024); [Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/new-ev-sales-down-26-8-since-last-year-used-sales-up)). *Verdict:* mostly outdated; the driveway, not the price, is the dividing line.

**"EVs are cheaper to own, full stop."** Only sometimes. AAA's 2026 study finds EVs at near parity with gas in SUVs, about 5% more in pickups, 29% more in mid-size sedans, and hybrids cheapest in every segment, because higher depreciation offsets the fuel and maintenance savings ([AAA, via Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/)). *Verdict:* not supported as a blanket statement; true for home-charging owners who buy used or keep the car a long time.

**"Nobody wants EVs anymore."** U.S. sales are down 30% so far in 2026 after the federal credit ended, yet the third quarter was the strongest of the year, 94% of EV drivers surveyed say they would choose an EV again, and global sales rose 20% in 2025 with Europe up 30% so far in 2026 ([Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-Kelley-Blue-Book-EV-Sales-Report-updated-as-of-10-13-26.pdf); [Plug In America, via EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/article/55391234/2026-ev-driver-survey-highlights-growing-satisfaction-and-charging-trends); [IEA, via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/05/19/iea-global-ev-sales-headed-for-another-record-year-despite-the-slowdown/); [Benchmark, via The EV Report](https://theevreport.com/global-ev-sales-september-2026)). *Verdict:* half true; the U.S. market shrank in 2026, the world's did not.

**"EVs are unsafe."** For their own occupants, a 2020 insurance-industry study found 22–41% fewer injury claims for EVs than their gasoline twins, and current EVs earn top crash ratings. The legitimate concern is weight: a vehicle 1,000 lb heavier raises the fatality risk for the people it hits by about 47%, and IIHS and the NTSB both flag it ([HLDI](https://www.iihs.org/media/ca2618fc-c875-4246-8a9f-5977f3b702f6/Ewxm_A/HLDI%20Research/Bulletins/hldi_bulletin_37-25.pdf); [IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/as-heavy-evs-proliferate-their-weight-may-be-a-drag-on-safety)). *Verdict:* not supported for occupants; partly true for everyone else on the road.

## How to read the verdicts

"Not supported" means the best available data point the other way. "Partly true" means the effect is real and smaller or more local than the claim. "Outdated" means it was true of earlier cars or an earlier grid. None of these is a verdict on any one car or household; the conditions that move the arithmetic are in part one.
