## Short version

A checking account is where pay lands and bills leave. Your banking app shows a balance, but that number only reflects what has reached the bank. It doesn't know about the check you wrote yesterday or the autopay due Friday, and it may count a deposit that could still bounce. Gas pumps and hotels can also hold more of your money than you spent. The fix is old-fashioned: keep your own running balance and subtract money when you promise it, not when the bank catches up.

## The idea in one sentence

The bank's balance is a snapshot of what has reached the bank; what you can really spend is that number minus everything you've already promised.

## How it works

**Posted, pending, current and available.** A posted transaction is final. A pending one has been approved but hasn't settled: a card purchase from this morning, say. Most apps show two numbers. The current (or ledger) balance counts what has posted; the [available balance](https://www.fdic.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/fil23019a.pdf) also counts pending charges, holds and deposits not yet final. It's usually what the app shows first, and it's the number many banks use to decide whether a purchase overdraws you.

**Holds on what you buy.** When you pay at a gas pump, the station doesn't know yet how much you'll pump, so it can place a temporary hold on your card. [Visa and Mastercard allow holds of up to $175](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/trending/visa-mastercard-raise-gas-station-debit-credit-card-hold-175/VSVXTOVKMFC2NOUMD5YKUJX6C4/). Hotels, rental cars and some restaurants do the same. The hold comes off when the real charge settles, within hours to a few days. On a credit card it ties up some of your limit. On a debit card it ties up your actual money, and it can cause an [overdraft](overdraft-fees.html).

**Holds on what you deposit.** A federal rule, Regulation CC, sets the longest a bank can usually make you wait. Cash deposited in person, direct deposits, government checks, and cashier's checks must generally be available the next business day. For other checks, the first [$275](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/files/bcreg20240513a1.pdf) is available the next business day and the rest generally by the second. Banks can hold money longer in some cases: more than $6,725 in checks in one day (only the amount over that), a check that bounced before, an account that's been overdrawn repeatedly, or a reason to doubt a check is good. Those holds [generally end by about the seventh business day](https://helpwithmybank.gov/help-topics/bank-accounts/funds-availability/funds-availability-exceptions.html), and the bank has to tell you why. Accounts open less than 30 days have [looser rules](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-229/subpart-B/section-229.13) and can be held longer. Checks deposited by phone are a gap: the rule's schedule [generally isn't read to cover mobile deposits](https://www.americascreditunions.org/node/23616), so your bank's own mobile deposit terms decide.

**Available doesn't mean good.** A bank has to let you use the money within a day or two, but finding out that a check is fake can take weeks. If it bounces, [the bank takes the money back from you](https://www.militaryconsumer.gov/node/75910), even after it looked cleared. This is how fake check scams work: someone sends a check, asks you to send part of it back by gift card, wire or payment app, and disappears.

**Checks you write.** The money stays in your account until the person you paid deposits the check, which could be tomorrow or months from now. Your app shows it as yours the whole time. A bank [doesn't have to pay a check more than six months old](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/4/4-404), but it's allowed to, so an old check you forgot about can still come through.

**Opening one.** Banks usually require an adult co-owner for anyone under 18. Many charge a monthly fee unless you keep a minimum balance or have direct deposit, so look for one that doesn't. People turned down because of past overdrafts are often flagged by a screening service called ChexSystems; many banks and credit unions offer "second chance" accounts for them. In 2023, [4.2% of U.S. households, about 5.6 million, had no bank account](https://fdic.gov/household-survey/2023-fdic-national-survey-unbanked-and-underbanked-households-executive-summary).

## A worked example

On Monday morning your app shows $420. Here's the week:

<table><thead><tr><th>Day</th><th>What happens</th><th>Your app shows (available)</th><th>What's really left</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Monday</td><td>You mail a $350 rent check</td><td>$420</td><td>$70</td></tr><tr><td>Tuesday</td><td>You pump $40 of gas; the pump holds $100</td><td>$320</td><td>$30</td></tr><tr><td>Wednesday</td><td>You buy $60 of groceries</td><td>$260</td><td>−$30</td></tr><tr><td>Thursday</td><td>Your landlord deposits the rent check</td><td>−$90</td><td>−$30</td></tr><tr><td>Friday</td><td>The gas hold drops to the real $40</td><td>−$30</td><td>−$30</td></tr></tbody></table>

On Wednesday the app said you had $260. You really had nothing left; the bank just didn't know it until Thursday. When the rent check arrives, the bank either pays it and charges an overdraft fee, [often $34 to $36 at large banks, though a few charge $10 or nothing](https://money.com/banks-overdraft-fees-increase-trump), or sends it back unpaid, which can mean a returned-check fee from the bank and a late or bounced-check fee from your landlord. Anything else that posts while you're negative can add more. With a running balance of your own, you'd have known on Monday that the week's limit was $70.

## What to do with it

**Keep a running balance.** A notes app, a spreadsheet, or the paper register that comes with checks. Subtract a check or an autopay the day you commit to it, and add deposits only once they're available.

**Turn on alerts** for a low balance and for every purchase over an amount you choose.

**At the gas pump, pay inside or use a credit card** if your checking balance is close to empty.

**Don't spend a deposit until it's available, and never send money back from a check a stranger sent you.** Wait for your bank to confirm the check has actually been paid.

**Once a month, match your count to the statement** ([reading statements](reading-your-statements.html) explains why it pays). Anything on the statement you don't recognize is worth a call.

**Decide about overdraft coverage on purpose.** For debit card purchases and ATM withdrawals, the bank can charge an overdraft fee only if you opted in; without it, most banks just decline the purchase, and [you can opt out](overdraft-fees.html). Checks and automatic payments can still overdraw an account either way.
