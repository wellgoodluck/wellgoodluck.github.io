## In one sentence

A housing headline reports one count of one kind of house at one point in the sale, and the first thing to find out is which.

## The analogy

A restaurant can count its evening three ways: reservations taken, tables seated, checks paid. All three describe the same night, they never match, and a slow week for reservations shows up in checks paid a few days later.

A house sale is counted the same way. A contract is signed, and a month or two later the sale closes. Some reports count signings and some count closings, some count only new houses and some only used ones. Figures below are for September 2026 unless noted.

## Six questions

**1. Which houses?** The National Association of Realtors counts existing homes, and it counts them when the sale closes: an annual rate of 3.99 million in September ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-increased-1-5-in-september-according-to-nar-report)). The Census Bureau counts newly built houses, and counts them earlier, when a deposit is taken or a contract is signed: an annual rate of 676,000 ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/pdf/newressales_202609.pdf)). "Pending" sales are contracts signed but not yet closed, so they lead the closings by a month or two. A headline that says "home sales" usually means one of the three.

**2. Is that how many sold?** No. Nobody sold 3.99 million houses in September. That is the month's sales, adjusted for the season and multiplied out to a year, a seasonally adjusted annual rate. The adjustment is there because home sales fall every winter and rise every spring, and without it every January would look like a collapse.

**3. Compared with when?** "Up 1.5%" was against August. "Down 2.2%" was against September a year earlier. Both were in the same NAR release. A record usually has a qualifier too: September's median price was the highest for any September, not the highest ever ([Associated Press](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/10/us-home-sales-rose-in-september-but-slowed-from-a-year-earlier-as-mortgage-rates-prices-rose/)).

**4. Is the median price the price of a house?** It is the price of the house in the middle of that month's sales. If more expensive houses sell this month, the median rises even if no house gained a dollar. And each source has its own middle: for September, $417,500 for existing homes from NAR, $393,895 from Redfin's sales ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/pending-home-sales-sink-rates/)), and $400,400 for new houses from the Census Bureau. An index that follows the same houses from one sale to the next avoids the problem, which is why the almanac's [home prices](../chart.html?id=home_prices) number uses one.

**5. How sure is the number?** The new-home figures come from a sample, and the Census Bureau prints the uncertainty beside each one. September's median new-home price was 3.9% below a year earlier, give or take 8.9 points, so the true change could have been a rise. The bureau says it takes four months to establish a trend in new-home sales ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/pdf/newressales_202609.pdf)).

**6. What does "months of supply" mean?** The homes for sale divided by the monthly pace of sales: how long the shelves would take to empty if nothing new were listed. Existing homes stood at 4.8 months and new houses at 8.4. There is no official line for a "balanced" market: Redfin uses four to five months ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/housing-market-update-pending-sales-lowest-level-since-2023)), and HousingWire reports that most economists use five to six ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/september-new-home-sales/)). The two supplies aren't alike, either. A new house can be for sale before it exists: of the 476,000 new houses for sale, 111,000 were finished, 250,000 were under construction and 115,000 had not been started, which by our arithmetic is about 2.0, 4.4 and 2.0 months of supply ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/pdf/newressales_202609.pdf)).

## Where the analogy breaks

A restaurant counts every check. Housing reports are estimates: the new-home numbers are a sample, the existing-home numbers are built from listing services and benchmarked later, and all of them are revised. Contracts fall through, too: 14.5% of the deals that went under contract in September were cancelled, by Redfin's count ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/pending-home-sales-sink-rates/)).

## What people argue about

**Median or index.** One side: the median is what buyers actually paid this month, in dollars anyone can understand. The other: it moves when the mix of houses moves, so it can rise in a falling market, and an index of repeat sales is the truer gauge. They measure different things, and most reports lead with the median because it arrives first.

**What "balanced" means.** Many economists use five to six months of supply as the line; Redfin uses four to five. A reader comparing two reports should check which line each one drew.
