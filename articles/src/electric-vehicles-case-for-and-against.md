## In one sentence

Whether an electric car pays off for a household turns mostly on two things the data keep pointing at: a place to plug in at night, which is where the fuel saving lives, and how long the car is kept, which is what lets the fuel and maintenance savings outrun the steeper loss of value.

## The analogy

An electric car is an annual pass. The pass costs more at the gate than a day ticket, and every visit after that costs a fraction of the day rate, so it pays for itself only if you go often enough and keep going long enough. A household that drives 15,000 miles a year, the figure AAA's cost study uses, and keeps the car past five years is a frequent visitor. One that drives little, or trades in after three years, bought an expensive ticket. {{check fig:ev_ratio <= 0.5 "the dek's 'far less per mile to fuel at home' and the analogy's 'a fraction of the day rate' assume home charging well under half of gasoline per mile"}}

Most figures below are as of late October 2026. The gasoline and electricity prices, and the sentences that depend on them, stay current.

## Where things stand

About one new vehicle in seventeen sold in the United States so far in 2026 runs on a battery alone, down from one in twelve in 2024. The drop traces to one date: the federal $7,500 purchase credit ended on September 30, 2025. Buyers rushed in beforehand and pulled back afterward. Worldwide the trend runs the other way: about one new car in four sold in 2025 was a plug-in.

**Cheaper models arrived.** The price premium over gas vehicles, about 11% in September 2026, is covered under the cost of ownership below. The under-$40,000 tier filled in during the past year: Tesla's Model 3 Standard ($36,990) and Model Y Standard ($39,990) arrived in October 2025 ([Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-surprises-with-dual-launch-of-new-model-3-and-model-y-standard-in-the-u-s-starting-at-36990/)); the 2027 Chevrolet Bolt lists at about $29,000 with 255 miles of range, and the 2026 Nissan Leaf starts at $31,485 with up to 303 miles ([InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/775349/2027-chevrolet-bolt-vs-2026-nissan-leaf/)).

**Used EVs are the fastest-moving corner of the market.** Used EV sales ran 28.8% ahead of a year earlier in February 2026, and the average used EV listed at $34,821, only $1,334 above the average used gas car, with 18 of 26 brands' used EVs cheaper than their gas equivalents ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/new-ev-sales-down-26-8-since-last-year-used-sales-up)). Prices in the under-$40,000 bands then rose 5–9% in the first half of 2026 as demand caught up with supply; Recurrent expects up to 500,000 EV lease returns in 2026 ([Axios, citing Recurrent](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/22/used-ev-prices-gas-demand)).

**Automakers are cutting big EVs and launching small ones.** Ford ended production of the all-electric F-150 Lightning in December 2025 with a $19.5 billion write-down, promising a range-extended version later ([Dealership Guy](https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/ford-kills-off-all-electric-f-150-lightning-as-part-of-massive-ev-course-correction-2025-12-16)). Honda cancelled three U.S.-built EVs in March 2026, Volvo is pulling the EX30 from the U.S. after 2026, and Hyundai skipped a 2026 Kona Electric ([S&P Global Mobility](https://www.mobilityglobal.com/en-us/automotive-insights/blog/ev-plans-us-cancellations-launches)); GM idled Cadillac EV production and cut shifts in late 2025 ([Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/gm-cuts-cadillac-lyriq-bolt-and-brightdrop-production-amid-slowing-demand/)). What is launching instead is compact and cheaper: the Bolt, the Leaf, Tesla's Standard trims, Rivian's R2 (mid-2026, $57,990 at launch), Lucid's sub-$50,000 Cosmos for 2027 and BMW's i3 for 2027.

**Owners mostly stay.** Over the 12 months to April 2026, 72% of EV owners who replaced their vehicle bought another EV; 18.5% went back to gas ([Experian](https://www.experian.com/content/dam/noindex/na/us/automotive/market-trends/2026/auto-market-trends-q2-2026.pdf)).

**Globally, growth continues.** More than 20 million plug-ins sold worldwide in 2025, up 20%, about one in four new cars; China accounted for roughly 13 million, over half its own market ([IEA Global EV Outlook 2026, via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/05/19/iea-global-ev-sales-headed-for-another-record-year-despite-the-slowdown/)). The IEA expects about 23 million in 2026; BloombergNEF expects 27% global share and a 19% U.S. decline ([BloombergNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/bloombergnefs-electric-vehicle-outlook-2026-global-ev-sales-set-for-another-record-breaking-year-but-growth-in-some-major-markets-slows/)). Through September 2026, Europe is up 30%, China down 13% and North America down 24% ([Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, via The EV Report](https://theevreport.com/global-ev-sales-september-2026)).

## The case for

The strongest arguments are the everyday ones: cheap fuel, little maintenance, and never visiting a station. The environmental case holds up under scrutiny, though the size of the benefit depends on where you plug in.

- **Fuel is cheaper at home.** Charging at home costs about {{fig ev_per_mile_home else="5.6¢"}} a mile at the average residential electricity price for {{fig ev_elec_month else="September 2026"}} ([BLS, via FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/APU000072610)); gasoline at {{v gas fmt=usd2 else="$4.27"}} a gallon ({{v gas period else="the week ending October 26, 2026"}}; [EIA, via FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GASREGW)) runs about {{fig ev_per_mile_gas else="15.8¢"}} a mile for a 27-mpg car, so a 15,000-mile year {{fig ev_saving_clause else="saves about $1,500 on fuel"}}. {{check fig:ev_saving >= 800 "the lead, the analogy and this bullet assume a large home-charging fuel saving"}}
- **Maintenance drops by more than a quarter.** No oil changes, no tune-ups, no exhaust or transmission to service, and regenerative braking means brake pads last far longer. AAA's 2026 study puts EV maintenance, repair and tires about 28% below gas for compact SUVs ([AAA, via AutoGuide](https://www.autoguide.com/auto/category/hybrids/even-with-high-gas-prices-it-s-still-not-cheaper-to-drive-an-ev-44640049)).
- **Refueling happens in the garage.** 86% of EV charging happens at home, overnight; owners with a driveway rarely see a public charger except on trips ([J.D. Power, via InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/791235/home-charging-jdpower-survey-study/)).
- **Batteries are outlasting the cars.** Fleet data show capacity loss averaging 1.8% a year and falling; Recurrent data reported by the Wall Street Journal put range retention at up to 95% after five years, and only 0.3% of EVs built since 2022 have needed a battery replacement ([Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/uk/press-release/2024-battery-degradation); [Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/research/how-long-do-ev-batteries-last); [WSJ, via Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/07/05/0434229/)).
- **Lower lifetime emissions on any U.S. grid.** An EV's battery makes it dirtier to build, roughly 30–100% more manufacturing emissions, but it repays that debt within about 13,000–21,000 miles on the U.S.-average grid and finishes its life 40–60% lower than a comparable gas car ([Argonne, via Reuters](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/reu20210629KCN2E50AP/); [MIT, 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/us-drivers-electric-vehicles-offer-emissions-benefits-and-cost-savings-0512)). On 2024 grid data the average EV is equivalent to a 100-mpg gasoline car nationally, and beats a 57-mpg Prius in regions covering 96% of the population ([Union of Concerned Scientists](https://blog.ucs.org/dave-reichmuth/epa-stopped-updating-the-data-behind-electric-vehicle-emissions-the-numbers-still-show-evs-are-winners/)).
- **Roughly 2.5 to 6 times more efficient.** EVs deliver more than 77% of grid energy to the wheels, including charging losses; gasoline engines deliver 12–30% of the fuel's energy ([fueleconomy.gov](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/evtech.shtml)).
- **Fewer fires and fewer injuries.** Sweden records 0.45 fires per 10,000 electric or hybrid cars against 7.5 for other cars; Norway 0.55 against 3.9 ([Swedish and Norwegian agency data, via Cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1074122)). A 2020 insurance-industry study of early EV and gas twin pairs found 22–41% fewer injury claims for the EVs ([HLDI](https://www.iihs.org/media/ca2618fc-c875-4246-8a9f-5977f3b702f6/Ewxm_A/HLDI%20Research/Bulletins/hldi_bulletin_37-25.pdf)).
- **Cleaner city air.** Zero tailpipe emissions, and regenerative braking cuts brake dust, the particulate most likely to become airborne, by about 83% ([EIT Urban Mobility, via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/05/27/another-way-electric-cars-clean-the-air-study-says-brake-dust-reduced-by-83/)).
- **The driving itself.** Instant torque, near silence, one-pedal driving and a low center of gravity are the reasons many owners give for not going back. That is a preference, not a measurement, but it is consistent across surveys: 94% of drivers in Plug In America's 2026 survey say they would choose an EV again ([Plug In America, via EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/article/55391234/2026-ev-driver-survey-highlights-growing-satisfaction-and-charging-trends)).

## The case against

Most of the real drawbacks are financial and logistical rather than technical. The two that matter most are depreciation and whether there is somewhere to plug in at night.

- **Depreciation is the biggest cost of ownership.** EVs lose 57% of their value in five years against 42% for the average vehicle and 35% for hybrids; that one line item wipes out most of the fuel and maintenance savings ([iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/cars-that-hold-their-value-study)). Fast-improving technology, heavy new-car discounting and the end of the federal credit all push used values down.
- **The sticker is still higher, and the incentives are mostly gone.** New EVs transact about 11% above gas vehicles; the $7,500 federal credit ended September 30, 2025, and state rebates remain only in some states, with California, Colorado, New Jersey and New York among the larger programs ([Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/sept-2026-atp-report/)).
- **No driveway, no deal, or close to it.** The fuel saving and the convenience both depend on home charging. Only about half of EV drivers who live in apartments have it, and public charging costs two to three times the home rate, which erases most of the fuel advantage ([Atlas EV Hub](https://www.atlasevhub.com/resource/the-state-of-electric-vehicle-charging-for-multifamily-housing/)).
- **Public charging still fails too often.** 12% of charging attempts ended without a charge in J.D. Power's 2026 survey, and a 2025 field study found a 71% first-try success rate; the newest networks are far better, the older ones worse ([J.D. Power, via EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/news/55398458/public-ev-charging-reliability-improves-as-newer-fast-charging-networks-raise-the-bar); [ChargerHelp](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250924845665/en)).
- **Road trips take longer.** The average 2026 EV needs about 16 minutes to add 100 miles at a fast charger; a gas fill-up takes five ([Recurrent](https://recurrentauto.com/research/fastest-charging-evs)). At a steady 75 mph most EVs fall about 12% short of their EPA range, cold weather takes roughly 20% off, and towing cuts range in half ([SAE and Car and Driver, via Autoblog](https://autos.yahoo.com/sae-paper-says-evs-worse-130000315.html); [Recurrent, via CarScoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/01/evs-lose-20-range-on-average-in-freezing-conditions-study-finds/); [Car and Driver](https://autos.yahoo.com/tested-ev-pickups-trailer-towing-130000822.html)).
- **Insurance and collision repair cost more.** New EVs run about 18% ($500) more to insure than comparable new gas cars, because repairs use more OEM parts and a damaged battery can total an otherwise fixable car ([Insurify](https://www.insurify.com/car-insurance/report/electric-vehicle-insurance-costs/); [Mitchell, via Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/08/21/mitchell-gap-in-average-repairable-claims-severity-between-bevs-ice-vehicles/)).
- **The battery is a rare but large repair.** Out-of-warranty replacement runs $5,000–$17,000. It is uncommon, under 4% of all EVs ever built and mostly first-generation cars, but the exposure is real on a 10-year-old car ([Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/research/how-long-do-ev-batteries-last); [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/articles/how-much-is-an-electric-car-battery)).
- **They are heavy.** EVs often weigh 20–30% more than comparable gas models, though some like-for-like pairs differ by only a few percent. Tires wear roughly 20% faster, and both IIHS and the NTSB have warned that the extra mass raises risk for occupants of lighter vehicles and for pedestrians ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/as-heavy-evs-proliferate-their-weight-may-be-a-drag-on-safety); [AP, via Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/us-official-warns-of-risks-posed-by-heavy-electric-vehicles/)).
- **Model churn.** In the past year the F-150 Lightning, Kona Electric, Niro EV, Volvo EX30, Honda Prologue, BMW i4 and others have been, or are being, discontinued or paused in the U.S. ([Business Insider, via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ev-graveyard-electric-cars-automakers-083301787.html)). An orphaned model can mean thinner parts and service support and weaker resale.
- **Extra fees and possible electrical work.** Forty-two states have enacted an annual EV registration fee, typically $100–$270 ([The ChargePort](https://thechargeport.com/ev-registration-fees-by-state)). A Level 2 charger is usually the largest single load in a house, and an older panel may need upgrading before one goes in; the costs are in the ownership section below.
- **Mining is front-loaded.** An EV needs about six times the mineral inputs of a gas car, and cobalt supply is concentrated in the Democratic Republic of Congo; cobalt-free LFP batteries are half the world market but under 10% of U.S. sales ([IEA, via S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/natural-gas/050621-iea-calls-for-government-action-to-ensure-supply-of-critical-battery-minerals); [IEA Global EV Outlook 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/electric-vehicle-batteries)). The counterweight is that those minerals are largely recoverable, while the 17,000 liters of gasoline a comparable car burns are not.
- **Hybrids are the cheaper answer right now.** On AAA's 2026 math, a conventional hybrid beats both gas and electric on total cost in every segment, with none of the charging logistics ([AAA, via Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/)).

## Charging: what is out there

Most EV charging never touches a public charger: 86% of it happens at home, overnight, according to J.D. Power's 2026 home-charging study ([J.D. Power, via InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/791235/home-charging-jdpower-survey-study/)). The public network exists for the other 14% (road trips, apartment dwellers and the occasional top-up), and it has nearly doubled since 2022, with fast-charging ports more than doubling, while getting measurably more reliable. It is still far from the gas-station experience.

**The three ways to charge.** Level 1 is a standard wall outlet: a few miles of range per hour, enough for a short commute. Level 2 runs on a 240-volt circuit like an electric dryer's and refills nearly any EV overnight; it is what most owners install at home. DC fast charging is the road-trip tool. The average 2026 EV peaks at 214 kW and adds 100 miles in about 16 minutes; the quickest 800-volt cars, the Hyundai Ioniq 5, Porsche Taycan and Lucid Air, do it in six to nine minutes ([Recurrent](https://recurrentauto.com/research/fastest-charging-evs)).

**How much exists.** The Department of Energy's station locator listed 250,406 public ports at 80,543 locations in June 2026, including 73,951 DC fast ports as of June 1 ([EV Infrastructure News](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/ev-networks/us-public-ev-charging-network-surpasses-250-000-charging-ports)). The independent analytics firm Paren counted more than 80,000 fast-charging ports by the end of the third quarter of 2026 ([Paren, via EV Infrastructure News](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/charging-installation/us-fast-charging-network-q3-2026-tesla-market-share)).

2025 was the biggest build year yet, with more than 18,000 fast-charging ports added, up from about 14,000 in 2024 ([Paren, via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/01/28/us-dc-fast-charger-deployment-saw-astounding-growth-in-2025/)); through the third quarter, 2026 deployments were running roughly 9–10% below that pace. {{due 2027-02-01 "Paren's full-year 2026 fast-charging deployment count (late January): update the build-year comparison"}} For scale, the U.S. has 122,620 convenience stores selling fuel ([NACS](https://www.convenience.org/storecount)) against about 80,500 charging locations, though a gas pump serves many more cars per hour, and most EV miles are charged in a garage.

**Who runs it.** Tesla operates roughly 37,000–38,700 U.S. fast-charging ports, about 49% of the total, down from 54% a year earlier because other networks are growing faster in percentage terms ([Paren, via EV Infrastructure News](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/charging-installation/us-fast-charging-network-q3-2026-tesla-market-share)). The connector question is settled: Stellantis gained Supercharger access on March 19, 2026, the last major holdout, so every major brand's EVs can now use Superchargers via adapter or a native Tesla-style (NACS/J3400) port ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/03/19/the-last-domino-falls-stellantis-evs-now-have-tesla-supercharger-access/)). Native NACS ports ship on 2025 and later Hyundai and Kia models, 2026 Toyota, Subaru, Lexus, Nissan Leaf, Rivian, Mercedes CLA and Porsche Cayenne, and 2027 GM, BMW, Volvo and Dodge models ([EVChargingStations.com](https://evchargingstations.com/chargingnews/all-evs-with-a-nacs-sae-j3400-charging-port/)). Behind Tesla: Electrify America (5,743 ports), EVgo with Pilot and Flying J (5,208), ChargePoint (4,848), Rivian (1,065) and Walmart (600 or more). The newest entrant, Ionna, owned by eight automakers, 400 kW, both plug types, targeting 30,000 ports by 2030, had about 1,190 ports in mid-2026, and Ionna and Walmart each installed more 400 kW-class ports than Tesla in the third quarter ([AFDC, via EVChargingStations](https://evchargingstations.com/chargingnews/dc-fast-charging-july-2026/); [Motor Illustrated](https://motorillustrated.com/ionna-transitions-from-beta-to-nationwide-ev-charging-network/148187/)).

**Does it work when you get there?** Better each year, from a poor start. J.D. **Without a driveway.** This is the real dividing line. About half of EV drivers living in apartments have home charging, against up to 94% of those in detached houses, and nearly a third of U.S. households are in multifamily buildings ([Atlas EV Hub, 2024](https://www.atlasevhub.com/resource/the-state-of-electric-vehicle-charging-for-multifamily-housing/)). **Maintenance.** AAA puts compact-SUV EV maintenance, repair and tires at $1,402 a year versus $1,954 for gas. **Insurance.** Insurify's June 2026 analysis of 235 million quotes found EVs averaging $3,159 a year for full coverage versus $2,218 for gas cars, 42% more, but that compares a young EV fleet with gas cars averaging 11 years old. **Fees and home equipment.** Forty-two states have enacted an extra annual registration fee on battery-electric vehicles (New Mexico's begins in 2027), median about $145: Texas $200, Georgia about $239, New Jersey $270 rising to $290 by 2028, California about $118; Nevada, Arizona, Florida, New York and Massachusetts charge none ([The ChargePort](https://thechargeport.com/ev-registration-fees-by-state); [ARTBA](https://transportationinvestment.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/State_Electric_Vehicle_Fees_April_2026.pdf)). ## Fifteen claims, checked

Claims heard in both directions, for and against, each checked against the best current data. Where a claim has a real basis, that is stated too.

**"You'll get stranded. Range anxiety."** The newest sub-$40,000 EVs are rated at 255–321 miles; 86% of charging is done at home; the U.S. has more than 80,000 public fast-charging ports and 12% of charging attempts fail, down from 19% in 2024. Rural counties lag: 45% had any fast charger in early 2025 versus 77% of metro counties ([J.D. Power](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/news/55398458/public-ev-charging-reliability-improves-as-newer-fast-charging-networks-raise-the-bar); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/ev-charging/map-rural-fast-chargers-trump)). *Verdict:* mostly outdated for daily driving with home charging; still real for rural road trips and renters.

**"The battery dies after a few years and costs $20,000."** Modern packs lose about 1.8% of capacity a year, and Recurrent data reported by the Wall Street Journal put range retention at up to 95% after five years. 0.3% of EVs built since 2022 have needed a replacement, versus about 1 in 12 built 2011–2016 (the early uncooled Nissan Leaf). Packs carry at least 8-year, 100,000-mile warranties; out-of-warranty replacement runs $5,000–$17,000 ([Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/uk/press-release/2024-battery-degradation); [Recurrent](https://www.recurrentauto.com/research/how-long-do-ev-batteries-last); [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/articles/how-much-is-an-electric-car-battery)). *Verdict:* not supported for current EVs; it was true of first-generation cars.

**"EVs catch fire more than gas cars."** Swedish, Norwegian and Tesla fleet data all show the opposite (Poland's 2025 figures are the outlier, at parity): Sweden 0.45 fires per 10,000 EVs against 7.5 for other cars; Norway 0.55 against 3.9; Tesla's fleet one fire per 140 million miles against one per 17 million for U.S. vehicles overall. But EV battery fires are harder to put out (3,000–8,000 gallons of water), can reignite, and saltwater flooding is a real trigger: 36 of the 3,000–5,000 EVs flooded by Hurricane Ian burned. In New York City, most lithium-battery fire deaths involve e-bikes and scooters, not cars ([Cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1074122); [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/767908/ev-fire-safety-data-2025/); [Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/VehicleFireSafetyReport); [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/weather/2024-10-11/after-hurricane-milton-growing-risk-flooded-electric-cars-going-up-flames); [FDNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/fdny/news/03-25/fdny-commissioner-robert-s-tucker-significant-progress-the-battle-against-lithium-ion-battery-fires)). *Verdict:* not supported on how often; true on how hard to extinguish.

**"EVs are dirtier than gas cars once you count the battery and coal power."** Building an EV emits 30–100% more than building a gas car; the debt is repaid in 13,500–21,300 miles on the U.S. grid and lifetime emissions end 40–60% lower. On 2024 grid data the average EV equals a 100-mpg gas car nationally; even the lowest-scoring grid region UCS reports (Michigan's, at 62 mpg-equivalent) beats the average new gas car. The claim was closer to true in 2013, when coal supplied roughly double its current share of about 16% of U.S. power ([Argonne, via Reuters](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/reu20210629KCN2E50AP/); [MIT 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/us-drivers-electric-vehicles-offer-emissions-benefits-and-cost-savings-0512); [UCS](https://blog.ucs.org/dave-reichmuth/epa-stopped-updating-the-data-behind-electric-vehicle-emissions-the-numbers-still-show-evs-are-winners/); [EIA](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/10/07/292552.htm)). *Verdict:* not supported nationally; partly true in a few coal-heavy regions, where a 57-mpg hybrid comes close.

**"The grid can't handle everyone charging."** EV charging used about 0.2% of U.S. electricity in 2023; data centers used 4.4% and are forecast at 7–12% by 2028. Full electrification of everything would raise 2050 demand 20–38%, at growth rates below the 1950–2016 average. In a California utility pilot, 85% of home chargers were already set to charge off-peak. The real constraint is local: a Level 2 charger is the biggest load in a house, and a street of them starting at 6 pm can overload a neighborhood transformer without staggered or time-of-use charging ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=62083); [LBNL](https://energyanalysis.lbl.gov/news/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers); [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy18osti/72096.pdf); [Peninsula Clean Energy](https://library.peninsulacleanenergy.com/m/91b5939732755c7/original/EV-Managed-Charging-Pilot-Final-Report.pdf)). *Verdict:* not supported at the national level; real at the neighborhood-transformer level.

**"California told people not to charge their EVs, proof the grid is failing."** The September 1, 2022 Flex Alert asked residents to shift all discretionary use (dishwashers, dryers, EVs) out of the 4–9 pm heat-wave peak. No outages occurred, and EV charging is under 1% of California's load even at peak ([NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/california-flex-alert-dont-charge-ev/2992997/); [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/greenvehicles/electric-vehicle-myths)). *Verdict:* misleading.

**"EVs don't work in the cold."** At or below 32°F, EVs lose about 20% of range on average (17% with a heat pump, 25% without), and fast charging slows unless the battery is preconditioned. The January 2024 Chicago incident involved near-empty cars that had not preconditioned, not failed chargers. Norway, where 96% of new cars sold in 2025 were electric, is the standing counterexample ([Recurrent, via CarScoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/01/evs-lose-20-range-on-average-in-freezing-conditions-study-finds/); [Consumer Guide](https://blog.consumerguide.com/makes/tesla/); [Reuters, via GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/motoring/971455/norway-s-new-car-sales-were-96-electric-in-2025/story/)). *Verdict:* partly true, a real penalty rather than a dealbreaker.

**"Hot climates ruin the battery."** Range drops about 5% at 90°F and 15% at 100°F, mostly from air conditioning. Geotab's data tie faster aging to heat combined with frequent fast charging, not to heat alone, and the fleet-wide gap between ideal and average conditions is about 0.2 points a year (1.6% against 1.8%); parking in shade and a lower daily charge limit are what the sources name as offsetting it ([Recurrent, via InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/763596/evs-best-summer-driving-range/); [Geotab, via Green Car Reports](https://www.greencarreports.com/news/1144490_study-never-mind-degradation-batteries-will-outlast-evs-in-most-cases)). *Verdict:* partly true, a small effect with modern thermal management.

**"EPA range numbers are fiction."** At a steady 75 mph, EVs average 12.5% below their label while gas cars beat theirs by 4%. In Edmunds' mixed real-world loop, most current EVs meet or beat their EPA rating, with GM, Hyundai and Kia, Mercedes and Porsche typically over and Rivian and Lucid under. Towing cuts range roughly in half, as it does for gas trucks ([SAE and Car and Driver, via Autoblog](https://autos.yahoo.com/sae-paper-says-evs-worse-130000315.html); [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/electric-car-range-and-consumption-epa-vs-edmunds.html); [Car and Driver](https://autos.yahoo.com/tested-ev-pickups-trailer-towing-130000822.html)). *Verdict:* partly true at interstate speed; not around town.

**"Batteries can't be recycled and end up in landfills."** The "only 5% recycled" figure is a 2016 collection statistic misread as a recycling rate; a 2023 estimate puts global lithium-ion recycling at about 59%, and most jurisdictions ban landfilling EV packs. Packs are worth thousands of dollars in metals; 73% of retired EV packs in 2023 went to second-life use first. The U.S. recycling industry's problem is too few dead batteries, not too many: Li-Cycle went bankrupt and Ascend Elements filed for Chapter 11, squeezed by scarce feedstock and falling metal prices ([Hannah Ritchie](https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/battery-recycling-myth); [Circular Energy Storage, via Auto Recycling World](https://autorecyclingworld.com/global-battery-recycling-volumes-to-rise-sharply-after-2030-new-data-from-ces/); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/recycling-renewables/li-cycles-quest-to-recycle-lithium-ion-batteries-ends-in-bankruptcy)). *Verdict:* not supported.

**"Battery mining is worse than drilling for oil."** An EV needs about six times the mineral inputs of a gas car, and cobalt sourcing is concentrated in the DRC. Over a car's life, though, roughly 30 kg of battery metals are lost after recycling versus about 17,000 liters of gasoline burned; the world extracts and burns about 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuels a year, roughly 500 times the critical-mineral tonnage needed ([IEA, via S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/natural-gas/050621-iea-calls-for-government-action-to-ensure-supply-of-critical-battery-minerals); [Transport & Environment](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/batteries-vs-oil-comparison-raw-material-needs); [Carbon Brief](https://interactive.carbonbrief.org/factcheck/electric-vehicles/)). *Verdict:* partly true; the mining burden is real, front-loaded and mostly recoverable.

**"EVs are toys for the wealthy."** The average new EV sells for $55,879 versus $50,562 for all vehicles, an 11% gap down from roughly 39% in 2022 by CarEdge's tally of Kelley Blue Book data. New EVs now start under $30,000 (Chevrolet Bolt) and $37,000 (Tesla Model 3); the average used EV lists at $34,821, $1,334 above the average used gas car, and 18 of 26 brands' used EVs are cheaper than their gas equivalents. The real divide is housing: homeowners with a driveway capture the savings, renters mostly do not ([Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/sept-2026-atp-report/); [CarEdge](https://caredge.com/guides/average-price-of-an-electric-car-2024); [Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/new-ev-sales-down-26-8-since-last-year-used-sales-up)). *Verdict:* mostly outdated; the driveway, not the price, is the dividing line.

**"EVs are cheaper to own, full stop."** Only sometimes. AAA's 2026 study finds EVs at near parity with gas in SUVs, about 5% more in pickups, 29% more in mid-size sedans, and hybrids cheapest in every segment, because higher depreciation offsets the fuel and maintenance savings ([AAA, via Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/)). *Verdict:* not supported as a blanket statement; true for home-charging owners who buy used or keep the car a long time.

**"Nobody wants EVs anymore."** U.S. sales are down 30% so far in 2026 after the federal credit ended, yet the third quarter was the strongest of the year, 94% of EV drivers surveyed say they would choose an EV again, and global sales rose 20% in 2025 with Europe up 30% so far in 2026 ([Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-Kelley-Blue-Book-EV-Sales-Report-updated-as-of-10-13-26.pdf); [Plug In America, via EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/electric-vehicles/article/55391234/2026-ev-driver-survey-highlights-growing-satisfaction-and-charging-trends); [IEA, via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/05/19/iea-global-ev-sales-headed-for-another-record-year-despite-the-slowdown/); [Benchmark, via The EV Report](https://theevreport.com/global-ev-sales-september-2026)). *Verdict:* half true; the U.S. market shrank in 2026, the world's did not.

**"EVs are unsafe."** For their own occupants, a 2020 insurance-industry study found 22–41% fewer injury claims for EVs than their gasoline twins, and current EVs earn top crash ratings. The legitimate concern is weight: a vehicle 1,000 lb heavier raises the fatality risk for the people it hits by about 47%, and IIHS and the NTSB both flag it ([HLDI](https://www.iihs.org/media/ca2618fc-c875-4246-8a9f-5977f3b702f6/Ewxm_A/HLDI%20Research/Bulletins/hldi_bulletin_37-25.pdf); [IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/as-heavy-evs-proliferate-their-weight-may-be-a-drag-on-safety)). *Verdict:* not supported for occupants; partly true for everyone else on the road.

## What the arithmetic turns on

The studies above agree on what widens the EV's margin and what erases it. None of it is a verdict on any one household; it is where the numbers come from.

The saving is largest when:

- charging happens at home on a 240-volt circuit, where a kilowatt-hour costs a third to a half of public rates (19.6¢ in September 2026 against the 42¢ and 54¢ public averages above);
- the car is driven a lot, since the saving accrues per mile;
- the car is kept well past five years: AAA's five-year window ends at roughly break-even, and the fuel and maintenance savings keep accruing each year after the steepest loss of value is behind the car ([AAA, via AutoGuide](https://www.autoguide.com/auto/category/hybrids/even-with-high-gas-prices-it-s-still-not-cheaper-to-drive-an-ev-44640049));
- the car is bought used, after the first owner has absorbed the steep early loss of value, at prices now close to used gas cars ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/new-ev-sales-down-26-8-since-last-year-used-sales-up));
- electricity is cheap relative to gasoline, as it is in most states outside the Northeast, California and Hawaii ([EIA state data, via ElectricRates](https://electricrates.org/states/report/2026-06/)), or the household charges on an off-peak schedule (owners who always schedule charging paid $65 a month against $71 for those who never do; [J.D. Power, via InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/791235/home-charging-jdpower-survey-study/));
- the climate is mild, or the car has a heat pump, which holds the average winter penalty near 17% ([Recurrent, via CarScoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/01/evs-lose-20-range-on-average-in-freezing-conditions-study-finds/)).

The saving narrows or disappears when:

- the car depends on public charging for everyday driving, at two to three times the home rate per kilowatt-hour (the charging section above);
- it is bought new and traded in every two or three years, so depreciation dominates ([iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/cars-that-hold-their-value-study));
- it regularly tows, or routinely covers long days on rural routes, where range and charger spacing both bite ([Car and Driver](https://autos.yahoo.com/tested-ev-pickups-trailer-towing-130000822.html); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/ev-charging/map-rural-fast-chargers-trump));
- the comparison is a mid-size sedan, where AAA's 2026 figures put the EV 29% above gas and a hybrid below both ([AAA, via Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/));
- the house has 100-amp service and no spare capacity, so a charger means a panel upgrade, $4,500–$8,500 all in by one installer's estimate ([Eviqo](https://eviqo.io/blogs/news/ev-charger-installation-cost-in-2026-complete-breakdown-by-state-setup-and-hidden-fees));
- the model's maker has recently cut or paused its EV lineup, which can thin parts and service support and weaken resale.

Three findings run through all of it: the fuel saving lives at home; the battery data tie faster wear to frequent fast charging in heat rather than to home charging; and rated range reads as a town figure, about 10–15% less at a steady 75 mph and about half when towing.

## Where the analogy breaks

An annual pass is worth nothing at year's end, and everyone knows that at the gate. A car is sold on, and here the EV gives back less than a gas car does: it resells at a steeper discount, so more of the premium paid up front is never recovered. A pass also makes each visit free; an EV's fuel is only cheaper, and by how much depends on where the car sleeps. At {{fig ev_elec_month else="September 2026"}}'s electricity price and the gasoline price for {{v gas period else="the week ending October 26, 2026"}}, home charging costs {{fig ev_ratio_words else="about a third of what gasoline costs"}} per mile; at a fast charger, in mid-2026, it cost about what gasoline did. The pass favors frequent visitors; the EV's arithmetic favors frequent drivers with a driveway.

## What people argue about

**Whether EVs are cheaper to own.** AAA's 2026 study, with no purchase credit, gasoline at $4.15 and electricity at 18 cents, finds near parity in SUVs, about 5% more in pickups and a 29% EV penalty in sedans, with hybrids cheapest everywhere ([AAA, via Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/)). Vincentric's 2025 analysis, run while the credit was in force, though only five of its 54 models qualified for it, found 44% of those models cheaper over five years than a comparable gas vehicle ([Auto Remarketing](https://autoremarketing.com/ar/analysis/study-finds-44-of-evs-cost-less-to-own-than-a-comparable-gas-vehicle)), and J.D. Power's 2024 work found EVs cheaper in nearly every state ([CBT News](https://www.cbtnews.com/j-d-power-reveals-evs-cost-less-to-own-over-5-years-compared-to-gas-cars-in-nearly-every-u-s-state)). The studies differ on the credit, on the gas price assumed, and on how much depreciation they charge. AAA's is the only one of the three run after the credit ended, and at a gas price near late October 2026's rather than 2025's $3.15; its depreciation figures come from a used market the study's own coverage describes as still adjusting.

**Whether heavy EVs make roads less safe.** IIHS and the NTSB's chair have said the extra mass of large EVs raises the risk for people in lighter vehicles and for pedestrians ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/as-heavy-evs-proliferate-their-weight-may-be-a-drag-on-safety)); the Center for Auto Safety, in the same AP report as the NTSB chair's remarks, cited a 2011 study in which being struck by a vehicle 1,000 pounds heavier raised the chance of death by 47% ([AP, via Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/us-official-warns-of-risks-posed-by-heavy-electric-vehicles/)). The counter-argument is that the gap depends on the pair: Electrek's check found a Tesla Model 3 and a BMW 3 Series about 5%, or 200 pounds, apart ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/05/27/another-way-electric-cars-clean-the-air-study-says-brake-dust-reduced-by-83/)), while the NTSB's examples were a Mustang Mach-E and a Volvo XC40 about 33% heavier than their gas twins and an F-150 Lightning 2,000–3,000 pounds heavier, and that EVs post fewer injury claims for their own occupants ([HLDI](https://www.iihs.org/media/ca2618fc-c875-4246-8a9f-5977f3b702f6/Ewxm_A/HLDI%20Research/Bulletins/hldi_bulletin_37-25.pdf)). None of the sources here isolates EV mass from the general trend toward heavier vehicles.

**Whether an EV beats a hybrid on emissions where power is coal-heavy.** On the lowest-scoring regional grid UCS reports, Michigan's at 62 mpg-equivalent, the average EV's operating emissions are close to, but still below, those of a 57-mpg hybrid; an EV using about a tenth more electricity per mile than the average would match the hybrid there ([UCS](https://blog.ucs.org/dave-reichmuth/epa-stopped-updating-the-data-behind-electric-vehicle-emissions-the-numbers-still-show-evs-are-winners/)). The same table supports two readings: the regional minimum is what matters for the households in that region; the population share, about 4%, is what UCS leads with. UCS's national figure has moved from 91 mpg-equivalent in its 2022 report to 100 on 2024 grid data as the grid changed ([UCS, 2022](https://www.ucs.org/resources/driving-cleaner)). Both readings are describing the same table.
