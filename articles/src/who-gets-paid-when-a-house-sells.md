## The short version

When a house sells, the price is not the only money that moves. On a $400,000 sale, the two real estate agents together typically collect the most, about $21,800 at the rates agents reported in mid-2026. The buyer's loan costs come next: the median borrower paid $6,684 in 2023, and that figure rose 37% in two years as more buyers paid points to lower their rate. Title insurance is smaller, about $2,262 for an owner's policy at the rate Texas sets, and the state's cut ranges from nothing in 16 states to $10,000 in Delaware. Two things surprised people who follow this. The 2024 legal settlement that was expected to push commissions down did not: the average buyer's agent rate was 2.36% when the new rules began and 2.42% a year later. And title insurers pay out very little of what they collect, about 3.6 cents of each premium dollar in 2025, which critics read as overpricing and the industry reads as the product working. Texas has ordered title rates down, and a federal program that lets some refinances skip the lender's title policy is being considered for the whole country.

## The analogy

The [gas page](what-sets-the-price-of-gas.html) follows a gallon past four toll booths. A house sale has four of its own. The price the buyer and seller agree on is the car; before it reaches the other side, it stops at the agents, the title and closing company, the lender, and the county and state. Each booth takes its toll out of the money on the table, some from the seller's proceeds and some from the buyer's cash.

The house on this page sells for $400,000, with 20% down and a $320,000 loan. Figures are as of November 2026.

## Booth one: the agents

Most sales involve two agents, one for the seller and one for the buyer, and each is paid a percentage of the price. Redfin, a brokerage that publishes the rate from its own closed sales, put the average buyer's agent commission at 2.42% in the third quarter of 2025 ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Press-Release_Q3-commissions.pdf)). On this house that is $9,680. A survey of 434 agents by Clever Real Estate, a referral company, in August 2026 put the listing side at 2.76% and the buyer side at 2.70%, or 5.46% together ([Clever](https://listwithclever.com/?p=10982)). On this house that is $21,840. The two sources measure differently, one from closed sales and one from what agents say they charge, and no public agency publishes a commission series.

For decades the seller's agent posted an offer of pay to the buyer's agent on the multiple listing service, the shared database of homes for sale. In October 2023 a federal jury in Missouri found that the National Association of Realtors and two brokerages had conspired to keep commissions high and awarded $1.78 billion. The association denied wrongdoing and settled in March 2024 for $418 million; the court gave final approval that November ([Real Estate In-Depth](https://realestateindepth.com/court-approves-418-million-nar-settlement-of-commission-class-action-case)). Two rules took effect on August 17, 2024: offers of pay to buyers' agents can no longer appear on the listing service, and a buyer must sign a written agreement with an agent before touring a home ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2024/03/15/nar-agrees-to-settle-commission-lawsuits-for-418m-nyt/)).

Many expected rates to fall. Redfin's average was 2.36% in the quarter the rules began, the low, and 2.42% a year later. Redfin's agents report that most sellers still pay the buyer's agent ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/real-estate-commissions-may-2025/)).

The commission is not the agent's take-home pay. Most agents work under a split with their brokerage: in the Realtors' 2024 member survey, 35% were on a fixed split, 20% on a split that rises with sales and 20% on one that is capped ([NAR](https://d7.nar.realtor/node/152913)). The agent's share has to cover the year's expenses and the hours spent on buyers who never buy and listings that never sell, since an agent is paid only when a sale closes. The association's members reported a median gross income from real estate of $59,200 in 2025, before median business expenses of $9,530. Members with two years or less in the business reported a median of $8,000; those with 16 years or more, $88,500 ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2026-member-profile-experience/)).

## Booth two: title and closing

Before a sale closes, someone searches the public records to confirm the seller owns the house and to find anything recorded against it: an unpaid tax bill, an old mortgage never released, a contractor's lien. Title insurance covers what the search missed. There are usually two policies. The lender's policy protects the lender; the owner's policy protects the buyer ([Iowa Title Guaranty](https://iowafinance.com/content/uploads/2024/09/Residential-NOA-1.pdf)). Each is paid once, at closing.

Prices vary by state. Texas sets one rate that every title company must charge. From March 1, 2026, an owner's policy on a $400,000 house there is $2,262, after the insurance commissioner ordered rates down 6.2% ([Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov//orders/documents/20259697.pdf)). Iowa is the outlier: a state agency, Iowa Title Guaranty, sells coverage on homes up to $750,000 for a flat $175, with the owner's coverage at no added charge when the lender buys its own ([Iowa Title Guaranty](https://cap.iowatitleguaranty.com/Public/ITGRateSheet.pdf)). The $175 leaves out the abstract and the attorney's opinion that Iowa's system requires, which the owner pays for separately, so the two numbers are not like for like ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/iowa-title-insurance-continues-to-thrive-under-state-run-model/)).

Title insurance is unlike other insurance in where the money goes. Insurers collected $18.5 billion in premiums in 2025 and paid $667 million in claims ([ALTA](https://alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/ALTA-Reports-2025-Market-Share-and-Title-Insurance-Premium-Volume)), a loss ratio of 3.6% ([Scotsman Guide](https://www.scotsmanguide.com/news/title-insurance-industry-posts-bumper-year-in-2025/)). When the Government Accountability Office looked at 2004 figures, title insurers' losses were about 5% of premiums against about 73% for property and casualty insurers, and about 71% of premiums went to title agents, mostly for searches, examinations and commissions ([GAO](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-06-568/html/GAOREPORTS-GAO-06-568.htm)).

Few companies stand behind the policies. The five largest underwriters wrote more than 75% of premiums in 2025: First American (23.1%), Fidelity National Title (14.5%), Old Republic (14.0%), Chicago Title (13.1%) and Stewart (10.9%). Fidelity National Title, Chicago Title and Commonwealth Land Title (3.3%) are brands of one company, Fidelity National Financial ([FNF](https://nationalagency.fnf.com/)), so four parent companies wrote at least 79%, by our addition of the association's figures.

## Booth three: the lender

Lenders report to the government what each borrower paid in total loan costs: origination fees, discount points, the appraisal, the credit report, and the title insurance the borrower pays for, so this booth overlaps the last one ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/junk-fees-are-driving-up-housing-costs-the-cfpb-wants-to-hear-from-you/)). The median for home purchase loans was $6,684 in 2023, up from $5,954 in 2022 and $4,889 in 2021 ([CFPB](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2023-mortgage-market-activity-and-trends_2024-12.pdf)). That is a rise of 37% in two years.

Much of the rise is points. A discount point is interest paid up front to lower the rate. As rates climbed, more than 56% of single-family loans made in 2023 carried some points, and the median amount on purchase loans was about $3,000 ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-report-finds-significant-drop-in-annual-mortgage-applications-and-originations-in-2023)). Loans insured by the Federal Housing Administration cost more at the start, a median of $11,368, because of the program's upfront insurance premium ([CFPB](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2023-mortgage-market-activity-and-trends_2024-12.pdf); [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/origination-fees-and-discount-points-soared-in-2023-cfpb)).

The appraisal is one of the smaller lines. Fannie Mae uses about $550 as the average a borrower saves when it accepts a loan without one ([Appraisal Institute](https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/insights-and-resources/insights/newsroom/ai-news-releases/2026-08-04-putting-fannie-maes-3billion-appraisal-savings)). [Four numbers for one house](four-numbers-for-one-house.html) covers what an appraisal is for.

## Booth four: the state and the county

Most states tax the transfer of a house, and the rates have little to do with one another. Sixteen states charge no state transfer tax, Texas among them. On a $400,000 sale, the state tax is $400 in Georgia (0.1%), $440 in California (0.11%, collected by counties), $2,800 in Florida (0.7%), $4,000 in Pennsylvania (1%) and $10,000 in Delaware (2.5%). Those are the state rates ([First American](https://first-exchange-ex1031.app.firstam.com/learn/articles/state-tax-rates-rules-real-estate-investing-1031-exchanges)). Counties and cities can add their own, and whether the buyer or the seller pays depends on the place ([PropertyShark](https://propertyshark.com/info/real-estate-transfer-taxes-by-state)).

Not everything paid at closing is a toll. Money set aside for the first year of property taxes and homeowner's insurance is the buyer's own, held by the lender to pay those bills later.

## What is changing

**Title rates in Texas.** The commissioner ordered a 10% cut in February 2025, a court blocked it, and after new hearings she ordered the 6.2% cut that took effect in March 2026 ([TDI, 2025](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/news/2025/tdi02062025.html); [TDI, on the injunction](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/TXINSUR/bulletins/3e4c842)).

**Refinances without a lender's policy.** Since 2024 Fannie Mae has tested accepting some low-risk refinances with an automated title review in place of a lender's title policy ([FHFA](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/statement/director-sandra-thompsons-statement-on-title-acceptance-pilot); [FHFA, questions and answers](https://fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Title-Acceptance-Pilot-FAQs.pdf)). An executive order of October 22, 2026, directs the regulator of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to expand that pilot into an ongoing nationwide program at both companies, if its director finds that sound. It covers refinances, not purchases ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/decreasing-housing-costs-for-the-american-people/); [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/trump-targets-mortgage-insurance-refi-costs-sweeping-housing-order)). {{due 2027-04-01 "Has FHFA made the title acceptance pilot permanent or widened it? Update What is changing"}}

**Fewer appraisals.** In early 2025 Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac began accepting purchase loans with down payments as small as 10% without a traditional appraisal, where their models allow it ([Inman](https://inman.com/2024/10/29/fannie-and-freddie-will-let-more-buyers-off-the-hook-for-an-appraisal/amp)).

## Where the analogy breaks

A highway toll is posted and the same for every car. Here the biggest toll is negotiable, the title toll is set by the state in some places and by the company in others, and who pays each one is itself part of the bargain: a seller who pays the buyer's agent may hold out for a higher price. And a toll booth sells nothing. Each of these collects for work, which is where the arguments start.

## What people argue about

**Whether the settlement changed anything.** One side: the Consumer Policy Center surveyed 281 buyers' agents in 26 metro areas in 2025 and found 95% still quoting 2.5% to 3%, and its authors call near-uniform rates evidence that agents are not competing on price ([BAM](https://nowbam.com/new-study-calls-out-price-fixing-patterns-among-buyer-agents-post-settlement/)). The Consumer Federation of America and the National Urban League reported in April 2026 that commissions had not fallen in a meaningful way ([CFA](https://consumerfed.org/reports/escalating-housing-costs-hidden-listings/)). The other side: the Realtors' association said in settling that it continues to believe it is not liable, and that pay would keep taking many forms, depending on what brokers and their clients negotiate ([NAR fact sheet](https://www.ncrealtors.org/031524-nar-settlement-factsheet/)). Redfin attributes the small rise since 2024 to a slowdown in sales that gave buyers more negotiating power ([The Title Report](https://www.thetitlereport.com/TTR/Articles/96218.aspx)).

**Whether title insurance costs too much.** One side: consumer advocates have long argued that insurance paying out about 5 cents on the dollar is overpriced; the Consumer Federation of America's insurance director told Congress in 2006 that the incentives to inflate its cost should be removed ([PropertyCasualty360](https://propertycasualty360.com/2006/05/07/congress-puts-title-insurers-on-hot-seat)). The GAO found that buyers rarely shop for a title company and rely on their agent or lender to pick one, so title companies market to those professionals instead of competing for buyers on price ([GAO](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-06-568/html/GAOREPORTS-GAO-06-568.htm)). The other side: the industry's trade group says the premium pays for finding and fixing problems before closing, and that this work is why claims are low. In a survey it published, title companies estimated that 36% of transactions in 2023 needed extensive, nonroutine work to clear the title ([ALTA](https://alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/Title-Professionals-Spend-Significant-Time-Resources-to-Protect-Property-Rights)). It says dropping the lender's policy on refinances shifts the risk without removing it ([ALTA](https://alta.org/topics/title-waiver-pilot)).

**Whether more of this should be optional.** Supporters of the title pilot and of appraisal waivers point to the cost: Fannie Mae estimates $3 billion saved on appraisals since 2018. The Appraisal Institute, an appraisers' group, says that estimate counts fees not charged and is not a measure of what borrowers saved ([Appraisal Institute](https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/insights-and-resources/insights/newsroom/ai-news-releases/2026-08-04-putting-fannie-maes-3billion-appraisal-savings)). The Appraisal Foundation, which sets appraisal standards, warned that wider waivers pose risks to consumers and the market ([Inman](https://inman.com/2024/10/29/fannie-and-freddie-will-let-more-buyers-off-the-hook-for-an-appraisal/amp)).
