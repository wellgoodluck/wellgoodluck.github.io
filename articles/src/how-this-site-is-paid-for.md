## What it costs

Two domain names, about $30 a year together, and research tools, about $200 a month: about $2,430 a year in all. Hosting is free: the pages are static files served by GitHub, the figures come from the St. Louis Fed's public data service, the email forwarding is free, and so is the weekly email, sent through Buttondown's free plan. There is no server, no database, and no bill for traffic. The other cost is time, which is considerable and unpaid.

## Who pays

One person, out of pocket. There is no sponsor, no institution behind it, and no business model.

## What it will not do

No ads, and no tracking of who you are: the site sets no cookies and loads nothing from an advertising company. No sponsored articles, no affiliate links, no paywall, no selling of anything. The numbers come from the agencies that publish them, with a link beside each one, and nothing on the site is placed there because someone paid for it.

## How visits are counted

The site counts page views with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com), an open-source counter run on donations. When a page loads, your browser sends GoatCounter the page's address, the site that linked you here if any, and your screen width; GoatCounter works out a rough location and the kind of browser from the request. It [never stores your IP address](https://www.goatcounter.com/help/sessions) and recognizes a returning visit for eight hours at most, so it cannot follow anyone from one day to the next or onto other sites. Charts embedded in other pages are not counted.

What the counts show is which pages get read and which sites send readers here. To stop your own visits being counted in a browser, open [this link](../#toggle-goatcounter) in it; opening it again turns counting back on. Ad blockers usually stop the count too.

## The weekly email

The [weekly digest](https://buttondown.com/EconomyInContext) is optional: one email on Monday mornings with the week's new articles and charts, the numbers that moved and what comes out next. It is sent through [Buttondown](https://buttondown.com), which keeps the list of addresses. Addresses are used to send the digest and for nothing else, and are never shared or sold. The emails carry no open or click tracking, every one has an unsubscribe link, and a reply reaches the editor. Past issues are [in the archive](https://buttondown.com/EconomyInContext/archive/).

## If you want to chip in

[Donations are taken on Ko‑fi](https://ko-fi.com/economyincontext), one-time or monthly. A donation is optional and changes nothing: no perks, no names, no influence over what gets written. Money goes to the costs above first; anything beyond that is thanks for the time.
