## How to use these pages

Each Money Traps page takes about five minutes to read and follows the same pattern: the short version, the trap in one sentence, a worked example in dollars, how to avoid it, and the policy argument around it, with each side's case. That last section makes the pages work for civics and economics classes as well as personal finance: students can argue either side from the page itself.

The pages, pictures and tools are free to use, print and share with credit under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Every page prints cleanly, without menus. The tools work on phones, need no account, and save and send nothing a student types.

Below, each page has two or three questions and one activity. Suggested order: start with credit scores, since several other pages build on it, then take them in any order.

## Credit scores: The Score You Didn't Know You Had

[Read the page](how-credit-scores-work.html)

- Which of the five parts of a score is most in your control at 18? Which is least?
- Why might a lender care how much of your card limit you use, not just whether you pay?
- **Activity:** Using the page's picture, rank the five factors by weight. Then list one action a new borrower could take for each.

## Overdraft fees: The $35 Coffee

[Read the page](overdraft-fees.html)

- In the worked example, the fees cost more than the shortfall. Why does a bank charge the same fee whether you're $4 short or $40 short?
- Who benefits when overdraft coverage is "on" for debit cards? Who benefits when it's off?
- **Activity:** Find the overdraft settings page for a real bank or credit union online, without logging in. What is the setting called, and what is the default?

## Credit card minimums: The Minimum Payment Trap

[Read the page](credit-card-minimum-payments.html)

- Why does the minimum payment get smaller as the balance falls, and why does that make payoff take longer?
- The page describes a federal rule requiring a payoff box on every statement. Has it worked? What would you add to it?
- **Activity:** In [How Long Will It Take to Pay Off a Card?](/tools/card-payoff.html), enter a $1,500 balance at the page's rate. Compare the minimum with $75 a month and $150 a month. How much does each extra $75 save?

## Buy now, pay later: Four Easy Payments, Times Four

[Read the page](buy-now-pay-later.html)

- Each plan on the page is interest-free. Where's the trap?
- Should these plans count on credit reports? Make the case for and against.
- **Activity:** Using the page's calendar picture, add a fifth plan of your own and find the week when the most payments come due.

## Payday loans and paycheck apps: The Two-Week Loan That Lasts All Year

[Read the page](payday-loans-and-paycheck-apps.html)

- A $45 fee on $300 doesn't sound like 391%. Explain the gap in your own words.
- Some states cap these loans and some don't. Using the page's argument section, which side do you find more convincing, and why?
- **Activity:** In [What a Payment Plan Really Costs](/tools/payment-plan-cost.html#k=payday), find the yearly rate on the example. Then try a $20 fee on a $100 loan for two weeks.

## Cash advances: Cash From Your Credit Card

[Read the page](credit-card-cash-advances.html)

- List the three ways a cash advance costs more than a purchase.
- Which "cash-like" purchases might count as cash advances without the buyer knowing?
- **Activity:** Using the page's timeline picture, explain to a partner why the purchase costs $0 and the advance about $50.

## Car loans: The Seven-Year Car Loan

[Read the page](long-car-loans.html)

- Why would a buyer choose a longer loan, knowing it costs more? Is that ever a good choice?
- What does it mean to owe more than the car is worth, and why does it matter if you need to sell?
- **Activity:** In [What a Car Loan Costs, by Length](/tools/car-loan-length.html), price a car from a real listing. Compare the 5-year and 7-year loans: the monthly difference and the total difference.

## Rent-to-own: The $38.99-a-Week Laptop

[Read the page](rent-to-own.html)

- Who uses rent-to-own, and what does it offer them that a store doesn't?
- Should stores have to show the total price on the tag? Use the page's argument section.
- **Activity:** Find a weekly price for a TV or laptop online (any rent-to-own store's site). Enter it in [What a Payment Plan Really Costs](/tools/payment-plan-cost.html#k=rto) with the same item's store price.

## Subscriptions: Ten Dollars Here, Fifteen There

[Read the page](subscription-creep.html)

- Why are subscriptions easy to start and harder to stop?
- Who should be responsible for reminding you before a free trial turns paid: you, the company or the law?
- **Activity:** List every subscription in your household you can think of, with its monthly price. Total it for a year. (Students can estimate; no real accounts needed.)

## Finfluencers and scams: Investing Tips From Your Feed

[Read the page](finfluencers-and-investment-scams.html)

- Name the four warning signs on the page's made-up post. Which is easiest to miss?
- If an influencer is paid to promote an investment, should they have to say so? Who should enforce it?
- **Activity:** Using the page's 1%-a-day picture, work out what a "guaranteed 1% a day" on $100 would be after a year. What does that tell you about the promise?

## The ad that sells the worry

[Read the page](ads-that-sell-the-worry.html)

- Name the four steps in the script. Find an ad you've seen recently that uses at least two of them.
- When is a free trial actually free?
- **Activity:** Pick one of the page's categories (supplements, debt relief, credit repair, Medicare plans, unclaimed money) and find the free or cheaper option the page names.

## The employer match: The Free Money You Leave on the Table

[Read the page](employer-retirement-match.html)

- Why would anyone not take free money from their employer?
- The page shows a saver who stops at 31 ending up close to one who starts at 32 and keeps going. Explain why.
- **Activity:** In [Are You Getting the Full Match?](/tools/employer-match.html), use a $40,000 salary and a match of 50% of the first 6%. What's left on the table at 2%? At 6%?

## Student loans: Borrowing for School

[Read the page](student-loans.html)

- Why does a balance grow while a student is still in school?
- The page describes rules that changed in July 2026. Who gains and who loses from the new limits?
- **Activity:** Using the page's worked example, estimate the monthly payment on the balance at graduation, then compare it with a starting salary in a job you're interested in.
