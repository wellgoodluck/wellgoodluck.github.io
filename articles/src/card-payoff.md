## How it's worked out

Each month, interest is the balance times one-twelfth of the yearly rate (APR). The payment goes first to that interest and then to the balance. The tool repeats this month by month until the balance reaches zero, and adds up the interest along the way.

The minimum payment is that month's interest plus a percent of the balance (1% unless you change it), and never less than a dollar floor ($25 unless you change it), a common formula. Card issuers use different ones; your card agreement gives yours. Because the minimum shrinks as the balance shrinks, paying only the minimum stretches payoff out. A fixed payment doesn't shrink.

It assumes you add no new charges, pay no fees and keep the same rate. The example's 22.36% is the average rate on card accounts being charged interest in the third quarter of 2026. Cards figure interest daily, so your statement's numbers can differ a little from these.

Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere. A link carries your numbers only if you copy one with them.
