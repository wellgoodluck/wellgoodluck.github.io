# Design System: Economy in Context

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Public Record"**

The site should look like something a fact-checker could put on screen without apology: neutral, citable, plain. Every visual decision serves one question a reader brings ("is this number high, low or normal?") and refuses to answer a second one ("is that good or bad?"). The look is a well-set page of warm off-white paper and near-black ink, one typeface, hairline rules, and a single teal accent that marks the reading itself. Color is spent on identity (which line is which), never on judgment.

The system has two registers. The almanac and the articles are **printed and plain**: text links with hairline underlines, tiles that read like boxed entries in a reference book, generous measure for reading. Chart pages are **quiet instruments**: compact segmented range controls, tabular numerals, an overlay panel of labeled toggles, a crosshair and tooltip. Both share the same tokens; the chart page simply packs them tighter.

Restraint is the identity. Confirmed rejections: no custom display typeface or brand color system, no motion or transitions, no shadows, no red/green or any value-judgment color on readings, no watermarks across charts, no dual y-axes.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Warm paper and near-black ink, light and dark schemes from the reader's system setting.
- One typeface (Public Sans, self-hosted, 400/600/700); hierarchy by size and weight.
- One accent, Neutral Ground Teal, for the data line, the latest-reading dot and the chart button.
- Flat surfaces separated by 1px hairlines; no elevation.
- Every chart carries its source credit inside the frame.

## Colors

A warm-neutral paper-and-ink palette with one teal accent and a reserved set of identity colors that appear only inside charts.

### Primary
- **Neutral Ground Teal** (light / dark in the frontmatter): the main data series, the latest-reading dot on range strips (hollow when the reading is outside its range), the focus ring and the tint of the "See the chart" button. Chosen because no party owns it; it must stay distinct from both party lanes.
- **Wayfinding Ochre** (`--nav`, #8a6100 light / #d9a83a dark; 5.5:1 on white, 8:1 on the dark sheet): navigation only, never data, so the rule is simple: teal is the numbers, ochre is how you get around. The section bar is tinted with it (`--nav-wash`, 9% light / 12% dark) under a 3px ochre rule; the section links are ochre text (the "ON THIS PAGE" label stays Ink Secondary, since it isn't a link), and the section in view turns ink over a 3px ochre marker (hover: ink). The other wayfinding links are ochre text with a 2px ochre underline: the site menu (The numbers · Your situation · Articles · About) on every page, the chart page's crumb and its phone dates line ("Change dates", "Reset"), the compared number's "Its own chart: {name} →" under a chart's key (Batch C), the Your situation links, "All on one page, with charts →" (on the All N numbers band), "↑ Top", "How to read this page", "How to read this", "Hide the overview" and "↑ At a glance". Strengthened Nov 5 (option D) after the first version, markers only, didn't register: the links' text stayed gray. Chosen Nov 5 over terracotta (too close to the first companion line in the Make your own chart picture, then called Chart to try) and plum (hard to tell from the teal for deuteranopes in dark mode, ΔE 4.6); ochre clears the teal by 12 (light) and 14 (dark) under color-blind simulation, and sits in the same warm family as the framing banner. **On the Hairline band** (Nov 10), where `--nav` is only 4.27:1, ochre text uses `--nav-band` (#805a00, 4.8:1 on the band and 6.2:1 on white; dark mode keeps #d9a83a, 6.4:1 on its band): the All N numbers band's link and the group bars' More control. ### Secondary
- **Companion slots 1–4**: the colors of optional companion series and sub-panel lines on chart pages: rust, violet, ochre and pink in light mode; orange, lavender, dark ochre and deep magenta in dark. Every pair, and each against the teal, stays apart under protanopia and deuteranopia (worst pair 12.9 light, 15.4 dark, OKLab ΔE ×100). A series keeps the slot it was given when it was turned on until it is turned off; four at a time at most. They never appear outside a chart or an article illustration (Nov 9), and never carry meaning beyond "which line" or "which one".

### Tertiary
- **Era lanes**: Democratic blue and Republican red as light tints (dark tints in dark mode) of equal lightness, so neither party looks heavier and the data line stays the loudest mark, with dark labels; a pale neutral for divided government; two alternating warm grays for Fed chairs and similar eras; a dark gray with white labels for wars, hatched with sheet-colored diagonals (label halo in the war gray) for a war Congress did not authorize that went past the War Powers Resolution's 60 days. Fed cycle and balance-sheet lanes use two neutrals, lighter for easing or expanding and darker for tightening or shrinking, so no traffic-light reading; the darker one is also hatched with sheet-colored diagonals, so the two differ by more than lightness, and a label on it gets a halo in that shade. A Fed segment too narrow for its name takes a short form: C and H for cutting and hiking, QE and QT for the balance sheet (never R, which reads as Republican beside the party lanes). Used only in the lanes under a chart, always labeled, and keyed in the legend whenever the color carries the meaning (Democratic, Republican, Divided; "Fed cutting rates / buying bonds", "Fed raising rates / shrinking its holdings", each with its short form in parentheses when one is drawn; "War authorized by Congress", "War not authorized, past 60 days").
- **Framing Banner** (warm cream with dark amber ink): the one tinted block, reserved for the framing line that appears whenever a political overlay is on.

### Neutral
- **Paper**: page background.
- **Sheet**: tiles, chart frames and other raised-by-border surfaces (white in light mode).
- **Ink**: headings, values, primary text; also the pressed range button (light mode) and the tooltip background.
- **Ink Secondary**: sentences, notes, captions, event markers.
- **Ink Muted**: dates, periods, axis labels, credits (4.5:1 on paper at its lightest; do not lighten it).
- **Hairline**: borders, row dividers, link underlines.
- **Range Bar**: the typical-range track on range strips; darker than a hairline because it carries meaning.
- **Frame** (`--frame`, the Range Bar's gray, Nov 6): the sides of every framed box: tiles, the home page's cards and index panel, the lead cards, the digest card, chart frames and article embeds. The Hairline alone read as too faint around a box on the light page. The Featured card keeps its 45% teal border; the 2px ink rule along the top of the lead and digest cards stays. Rows, dividers and link underlines stay Hairline (or Gridline).
- **Gridline**: chart grid, deliberately quieter than the hairline.
- **Reference Gray**: dashed reference lines and their labels.

### Named Rules
**The Identity-Not-Judgment Rule.** Color says which, never whether. No good/bad, up/down or red/green coloring of any reading, anywhere.

**The Neutral Ground Rule.** The data line never takes a party's hue. Companion colors avoid blue, red, teal and green: those belong to the parties and the main series.

**The Four Lines Rule.** A chart carries at most four companion or sub-panel lines, each in its own slot color, and a line keeps its color while it is on. A fifth waits until one is turned off; colors are never reused on one chart.

**The Three Copies Rule.** The tokens are copied in `index.html`, `chart.html` and `assets/site.css`. Change all three together, in both schemes. (`--frame` was added to all three on Nov 6; `chart.html` took `--nav` and `--full-ink` the same day, with the values the other two already had, for the site menu and the Copy image button.) The images made under Use this chart are drawn in the light tokens, and "Black and white, for print" swaps in its own grays (the `export-bw` rules in `chart.html`); those exist only in `chart.html`, because only it makes images.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Public Sans (with system-ui, -apple-system, Segoe UI, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Public Sans
**Label/Mono Font:** Public Sans with tabular numerals for figures in charts and tables

**Character:** A sturdy public-sector grotesque, open and unfussy; it reads as institutional without being cold, and its tabular figures keep columns of numbers honest.

### Scale
The almanac and chart pages use one scale: **11 · 12.5 · 13.5 · 15 · 16 (base) · 17 · 19 · 22 · 28 · 40 · 44px**, with four steps between 11 and 15. Chart text is 11px everywhere inside an SVG (axes, lanes, events, credits), except range-strip labels, which are 12.5px: they are the context the site is named for, so they are never the smallest text on the page. The images made for download and social posts have their own sizes, set for the image's frame. Article pages (`assets/site.css`) use the same scale from 17px body text: the title 40px (28px on phones), the dek 19px (17px), section heads 22px, list titles 22px, captions, datelines and footers 13.5px, small labels 12.5px.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (700, 40px; 28px on phones, 1.1): the site name on the almanac masthead, set as the lockup (see Masthead): "Economy" in 700, "in Context" in 400 Ink Secondary, an ink period. A chart page's indicator name is 28px at every width, so the reading stays the largest thing on screen.
- **Reading** (600, 40px on tiles, 44px on chart pages, 1.0, tight tracking): the latest value of an indicator. The largest number on any screen is always the reading.
- **Headline** (700, clamp 28–40px, 1.12): article titles.
- **Title** (700, 22px, 1.2): section and article h2 headings; the "At a glance" heading.
- **Tile title** (700, 19px, 1.25): indicator names on tiles.
- **Body** (400, 17px, 1.55, 39em measure): article text. The almanac runs at 16px/1.5.
- **Context** (600, 15px): the one-sentence verdict under a range strip ("Higher than 67% of months since 1948").
- **Meta** (400, 13.5px, Ink Secondary or Muted): subtitles, periods, notes, sources, controls (range buttons, custom range, actions), tables.
- **Caption** (400, 12.5px): legends, the hover box, overlay notes, counts, print footers.
- **Label** (600, 12.5px, 0.05em, uppercase): "Events in view" and similar small labels (the overview's group headings moved up to 13.5px/700 ink on a band, Nov 5); the Featured label uses Ink Secondary and 0.06em.
- **Chart text** (400, 11px, tabular numerals): axes, lane labels, event labels and numbers, credits. Range-strip labels are 12.5px.

### Named Rules
**The Reading Is Loudest Rule.** On any screen the latest value is the largest, heaviest number. Nothing decorative competes with it.

## Layout

A single centered column, max 1120px, with a 16px side gutter at every width. The home page (the default view since Nov 6) runs, in order: masthead, In short (with the Updates box beside it on desktop), the Featured strip, Six numbers for a household, Your situation, the lead cards, All N numbers (N the number of tiles), the weekly digest and a short footer; on phones the Featured strip comes before In short; see Home page below. (Nov 9: the lead cards moved up from after All N numbers, and the strip was added, after a measurement put the Featured card 5.8 phone screens down.) The full almanac moved to `?view=all` unchanged. It opens with the masthead, a section nav, the "At a glance" overview (each group a full-width band with its compact rows in three columns under it on desktop, two at tablet width, one on phones), then indicator tiles two to a row with a 16px gap. In a section with an odd number of tiles, the designated wide tile spans the row at ≥761px: reading and notes on the left (5/12), a taller 300px chart on the right (7/12).

Articles hold text to a 39em measure (about 76 characters at 17px); embedded charts break out to 860px. Chart pages place the chart and its controls left and a sticky overlay panel right at ≥861px, with Use this chart after the readings disclosure in the left column; below that the panel sits right under the chart's key and framing line and Use this chart follows the panel (then the events list, note and source, and the readings), with a jump row between chart and key ("Overlays · N on ↓" and "Copy or share ↓", see Navigation) and a bar at the bottom of the screen that leads back to the chart while the panel is in view and the chart isn't.

The section nav, a labeled bar on the full almanac (`?view=all`) and the articles index alike, sticks to the top at every width (the home page has none); at ≤760px it becomes one sideways-scrolling row with 46px targets. Anchor targets carry a scroll margin of the nav's measured height so jumps land below it, and range buttons grow to about 36px tall. Strips and charts reserve their height before drawing so the page doesn't jump.

Print hides navigation and controls, keeps tiles and charts whole, and ends with the domain.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat. There are no shadows anywhere in the system. Depth is tonal and structural only: white sheets on warm paper (dark: slightly lifted charcoal on near-black), separated by 1px hairlines. The pressed range button and the tooltip invert to ink, which is the only "raised" state; in dark mode the pressed button fills with the Range Bar gray instead, with ink text in 600, because an ink block glares on the dark sheet.

### Named Rules
**The Paper Rule.** Surfaces are flat. A border separates; nothing floats.

## Shapes

Gently squared. Three radii: 2px for small swatches and focus outlines, 4px for buttons, the segmented range control, the framing banner and the tooltip, and 6px for tiles, chart frames and article embeds. Borders are 1px hairlines. Chart marks are round-capped 2px lines (1.6px for companions), round dots with a 2px sheet-colored ring, and small triangles for event markers and outside-the-range arrows.

### Range Strip (signature component)
The site's most original element, and a ranking rather than a ruler. The track (Range Bar) spans an indicator's typical range, the 5th to the 95th percentile of its own history; the latest reading is a teal dot with a sheet-colored ring placed at its rank, so it always agrees with the sentence beneath it; the 2015–19 average is a tick in Ink Secondary placed at that average's own rank, drawn over the dot when the two meet so it is never hidden. The end labels are the readings at the 5th and 95th percentiles. The center of the track is the median by construction; a short Muted notch marks it, and the label above names it in the longest form that fits between the end labels ("typical range · median 4.2%", else "median 4.2%"; on price tiles "typical range, in today's dollars · median $3.27", else "in today's dollars · median $3.27", else "in today's dollars", since the qualifier outranks the median). Naming the median is what shows the bar is a ranking, not a ruler. A reading outside the range keeps the dot, pinned at that end of the track but hollow (Sheet fill, teal ring), with an arrow outside it pointing outward and, on tiles, the label "below/above the range" under the track at that end, on the 2015–19 label's line (moved off the track Nov 5, where it read like struck-through text; when the two labels would touch it steps past the average's label, or the average's label steps aside to start or end at its tick), both in Ink Secondary, the 2015–19 tick's gray: the hollow dot is still the reading, in the reading's color, and the arrow and label are an annotation, so they wear the annotation color (chosen Nov 3 from mocks; ink was tried and was the heaviest mark on the page in dark mode). The dot stays because its absence was the easiest thing on the page to miss, and outside the range is the one state a reader should stop at. On tiles it carries end labels and the average's value; in the overview it is compact and unlabeled, with the full sentence in its accessible name. Its own accessible name says whether the reading is within, below or above the range and gives the average. Price indicators use the range in today's dollars.

### Embeds
An article's frame has one height for every screen. On this site's own pages the embedded chart grows its frame to fit its content (never shrinks it); in a frame on another site it gives up plot height instead, down to 120px. The embed code still sizes the frame from sub-panels, lanes, framing and events.

Every chromeless view is `chart.html?id=<id>&…&embed=1` (articles' figures and both embed codes): no masthead, site menu, crumb, controls, panel, jump row, dates line or Use this chart, and no skip links; the head line "{Name}: {what it measures}" with "Full chart and context →" leads to the full page. **Two modes (Batch B):** an embed that stays current is the view as it is; **an embed frozen at today's data** adds `&asof=` with the date of the latest reading when the code was copied (the month for monthly, quarterly and yearly series, the day for weekly ones). The frozen embed cuts the indicator and its companions at that date, shows the last reading before it as its headline, and in place of the context sentence (which describes today's reading) says "Readings through {period}, as embedded. Newer readings are on the full chart." It freezes which readings appear, not their values: the data is always the current vintage, so later revisions by the agencies still show. A chart page ignores `asof`.

### Show it as (Batch C, Nov 6)
At the top of the overlay panel, after "On now": the forms a chart can be drawn in, as radios (18px, ink accent, never the browser's blue). The tile's own form is first and the default; then, in this order, whichever of these is not the tile's own: **Change from a year ago**, **As reported** (the level in the series' own unit, named for it: "Dollars an hour, as paid", "Index, 1982–84 = 100, as reported"; price pages "Dollars a gallon, as charged"; rates "Rate, as reported"), **In today's dollars** (named for the unit: "Dollars an hour, in today's dollars") and **Index, 100 at the start of the view**. Under them a helper line, 12.5px Ink Secondary: "Changes the chart and what you copy or download. The reading, bar and sentence above stay as the tile shows them." Desktop: an open fieldset under its 15px/600 legend, rows 6px apart, a hairline above. Phones and tablets (≤860px, where the panel sits under the chart): one 44px row, "Show it as" (15px/600) at the left and the current choice at the right in 13.5px Ink Secondary with "▸" ("▾" open), a button with `aria-expanded` that opens the same radios in place, each a 44px row; the legend stays for screen readers. The narrow-screen rules come after the base rules in the style sheet, so nothing meant for one width shows at another.

- **Disabled, not hidden.** A form a tile can't take stays in the list, its radio disabled and its name in Muted, followed by the reason in Muted: "· not for rates", "· not for shares", "· not for ratios", "· not in dollars", "· an index, not dollars", "· already adjusted for inflation", "· not for a balance", or "· not available yet" (the site doesn't keep that series yet; since Batch E, Nov 6, this shows only when a level's companion is missing from `data/companions.json`, as in the minutes after a deploy before Refresh writes it). A disabled radio is inert, so it is out of the tab order and arrow keys skip it; its reason is part of its label.
- **The rules.** Rates, shares and ratios (unemployment, yields, the mortgage rate, delinquency, the deficit and debt as shares of GDP, the electric-mile ratio, openings per job seeker) never get today's dollars or an index, and their change from a year ago is a difference in **percentage points**, said in the option's name ("Change from a year ago, in percentage points"), the unit ("percentage points, change from a year earlier") and the ticks ("2 pts"). Counts and prices change in percent; a balance (trade) and a count already in changes (jobs) change in their own unit. A weekly series' year-ago change uses the same week a year earlier (52 weeks back, within three days); monthly, quarterly and yearly ones the same period a year earlier; a missing year-ago reading (October 2025 was never published for the household survey and the CPI) leaves a gap rather than a guess. Jobs added over a year is the sum of twelve monthly changes, which is the level's change from a year ago. Today's dollars use the method of the price pages (`deflate()` in `fetch_fred.py`: dollars of the latest reading's month, by the CPI; a price page uses its own `_real` line). The index is 100 at the first reading in view, so it moves when the range does.
- **What it changes:** the chart (its line, axis ticks, a unit caption over the plot in 11px Ink Secondary, the hover box), its key (the main entry names the unit drawn), the readings table, and everything Use this chart makes: images (SVG, PNG, PDF), both CSVs, the alt text, the citations' chart name and permalink, and both embeds. **What it doesn't:** the headline reading, the previous and year-ago line, the range strip and the sentence, which describe the tile. So whenever another form is shown a line over the chart says so, 13.5px Ink: "The chart shows “{form}.” The reading, bar and sentence above show “{own form}.”" (in an embed "The reading above shows …", in an image "The latest reading above is …", in 600 under the subtitle or as the note under the sentence). New templates that quote a form name use U.S. punctuation: a period or comma after the quoted name goes inside the closing quote ("The chart now shows “Dollars an hour, as paid.”", alt text "…shown as “Dollars an hour, as paid,” January 2018 to …"); a label that ends without punctuation stays as it is ("Only for “Change from a year ago”"), plus "In this form the figures start in {Month YYYY}, as far back as the site keeps them." when a level is read from a series the site keeps from a later month than the tile's own readings (since Batch E every level starts at or before them, so the note shows only while a companion still holds the old 2018 history); the price pages' "as charged" note gives way to it.
- **A form that starts later** than the range chosen starts the chart at its first reading (the range buttons keep their state, the dates line shows the dates drawn).
- **Reference lines:** targets and estimates (the Fed's 2%, the FOMC longer-run rates, the CBO projection, the 1946 peak, the Sahm threshold) show only in the tile's own form; the median and the 2015–19 average are worked out again from the readings drawn (the median "since {the month they begin}", the average only when they cover 2015–19) and rest on an index. A price page's today's-dollars line shows only in its own form. Companion series stay on: on the main scale in the tile's own form, and each in a sub-panel of its own in any other (they are in the tile's own unit).

**The tile × form matrix** (since Batch E, Nov 6: the fetcher keeps each level from its series' first month, `pay_level` from 1964, `rent_level` from 1981, `home_price_level` from 1991, and four levels added for this, `core_cpi_index` CPILFESL from 1957, `payrolls_level` PAYEMS from 1939, `health_jobs_level` CES6562000101 from 1990, `gdp_real_level` GDPC1 from 1947, all `"for": []`): rates and shares (unemployment, Fed funds, 10-year, mortgage rate, delinquency, deficit, debt, electric vs. gas mile) and openings per job seeker: as reported (own) and change from a year ago; claims, housing starts, newspaper jobs: own, change from a year ago, index; the nine price pages: own (as charged), change from a year ago, today's dollars, index; trade: own, change, today's dollars; hourly pay: own (change from a year ago), as paid, today's dollars, index, from January 1964; inflation (CPI), core inflation, rent and home prices: own, the index as reported, index (core from 1957, rent from 1981, home prices from 1991); jobs added: own, change from a year ago, total jobs as reported, index; health care jobs: own, as reported, index; economic growth: own, change from a year ago (each quarter's real GDP, GDPC1, against the same quarter a year earlier; its CSV says so and names GDPC1), real GDP as reported (trillions of 2017 dollars on the page, the CSV in GDPC1's billions of chained 2017 dollars), index. Total jobs and health care jobs as reported are shown in millions, one decimal ("159.3M"; whole millions on the ticks), unit "millions"; their CSVs keep the raw thousands and say "thousands of jobs" (the CSV_UNIT rule, through `lvlCsvUnit`); the tiles' own forms keep K. **A level with a name of its own** (`lvlName` in SHOW_AS: Total jobs, Health care jobs, Real GDP) goes by it, for the level and its index, in the key, the readings table, the CSV first line and header, the citation and the alt text ("Total jobs (millions)", "Total jobs, as reported (thousands of jobs)", "Real GDP (index, 100 at Q3 2001)"), never "{Title}, {name}, as reported"; the image keeps the tile's title, with the form line under it. **Source line:** while a form is drawn from a series other than the tile's (GDP's real GDP, index and change from a year ago, from GDPC1), the Source line under the chart, on the page and in an embed, names it too: "Source: FRED A191RL1Q225SBEA; real GDP from FRED GDPC1, updated …". Ruled out as before: today's dollars for indexes, counts and GDP; index and today's dollars for rates, shares and ratios; index for a balance. No form is "not available yet" on the committed data. The full table is in the Batch E wording sheet (Batch C's for the reasons).

### Compare with another number (Batch C)
At the foot of Companion series, under a hairline: the label "Compare with another number" (13.5px Ink Secondary), a select (Sheet, 1px Muted border so the control passes 3:1, 4px radius, 38px tall, 44px and 16px type on phones) whose placeholder is "Choose a number…" and whose options are every other tile, by its name as on the home page's index, in optgroups named for the almanac's sections; "Stop comparing" beside it while a number is compared (a text button, 44px on phones); then the rule in one Muted line, "Numbers measured the same way share this scale; any other opens in its own panel under the chart.", and where the number went ("On the same scale as the chart: both are in …", or "In its own panel under the chart: … is in …, the chart in …"). One comparison at a time; it takes a color slot like a companion (the Four Lines Rule counts it, and the select rests while four lines are on).

- **The unit rule.** The compared number is always drawn in its own form. Each form carries a unit key (how it is measured and in what unit: "percent, year over year", "percent" as a level, "dollars per gallon" as charged, "percentage points, change from a year earlier", an index, …). When the compared number's key matches the main line's as currently drawn (after Show it as), it is another line on the main scale in its slot color, keyed "Compared: {name} ({unit})". Otherwise it gets a sub-panel under the chart, titled "Compared: {name} ({unit})", with the main chart's x-axis and dates, its own y-axis in its own number format, and the main plot gains its unit caption so the two scales are told apart. **Never a second axis on one plot.** When Show it as changes, the comparison moves between the main scale and its panel; it is never turned off by it.
- Under the key, 13.5px Ink Secondary: "Compared with {name}, on the same scale: both are in {unit}." or "Compared with {name}, in its own panel under the chart, with its own scale ({unit})." and an ochre wayfinding link to its chart page, "Its own chart: {name} →".
- In the readings table and the data-in-view CSV it gets its own column, "Compared: {name} ({unit})" (in a CSV the raw numbers' unit, as below), matched to the indicator's periods: the same week (within three days) for two weekly numbers, the period's reading for a coarser one, and for a number reported more often than the indicator the average of its readings in each period ("…, average for each month"). Images draw its line or panel and key it; the alt text, the citations' name ("Hourly pay compared with inflation, …") and its last reading say so.

### CSV units (Batch C fix)
A CSV holds the raw numbers, so every header names their unit, not the scaled one the page shows: electricity's own `csv_unit` (dollars per kilowatt-hour where the page shows cents), trade in millions of dollars (shown as billions), housing starts in thousands of units per year (shown as millions); claims are whole counts either way. The same raw unit carries into the forms made from them ("millions of dollars, change from a year earlier", "millions of dollars, in today’s dollars") and into a compared number's column. The two overrides are `CSV_UNIT` in `chart.html`; a new tile shown at a scale other than 1 needs a `csv_unit` or an entry there.

### Chart page addresses (Batch C)
A chart's view lives in its address: `range=` (1y, 5y, 10y, 50y, all; 25 years when absent), or `from=` and `to=` (YYYY-MM), `ov=` (overlays, series in color order; `none` keeps a price page's default line off), **`as=`** (Show it as: `yoy`, `level`, `real` or `index`; absent for the tile's own form) and **`cmp=`** (the id of the compared tile). In an embed also `id=`, `embed=1` and `asof=`. An unknown or unavailable value is dropped silently (`as=real` on the unemployment rate draws the rate as reported; `cmp=` naming the page's own tile or no tile is ignored). Copy link, both embeds and the citations' permalinks carry `as=` and `cmp=`; a frozen embed makes its forms from the full history, then stops them at `asof`, so today's dollars stay today's.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** keep the reading the largest number on the screen (40px tiles, 44px chart pages).
- **Do** give every number its period and source, including in downloads, embeds and printed pages.
- **Do** label every color: legends for series, text in every lane segment wide enough to hold it, a legend key for lane colors that carry meaning, hover titles for the rest.
- **Do** check every change in both color schemes at 1280px and 375px, with contrast computed: text ≥4.5:1, meaningful marks ≥3:1 or backed by labels.
- **Do** put a compared number, or a companion that Show it as moved off the main scale, in its own sub-panel under the main chart when it isn't measured the way the main line is drawn; share the scale only when the unit key matches.
- **Do** put different scales in a stacked sub-panel under the main chart. Lines measured the same way (the metro areas on the health care chart, each a percent change) share one sub-panel and one scale, named for the measure, and get their own heading in the overlay panel.
- **Do** let a sub-panel's name wrap to the plot's width, and a long name in a downloaded image's key wrap under itself; nothing runs off the chart.
- **Do** place anything on a range strip by rank, never by value, so the picture and the sentence agree.
- **Do** say "as charged" wherever a price is drawn as charged beside a range or sentence in today's dollars.
- **Do** give the median and the 2015–19 average different line patterns (dotted and dashed), keyed in the legend.
- **Do** show a deficit the way it is reported: the federal deficit as a positive number (FRED's sign flipped, and the note and CSV say so), so "5.8%" reads as it sounds. A balance that can go either way (trade) keeps its sign, says "balance" in its title and "below zero is a deficit" in its subtitle. Either way the sentence says "a larger/smaller deficit", never "higher/lower".
- **Do** change tokens in all three files at once.
- **Do** give every framed box Frame sides (the Featured card keeps its teal), and keep rows and dividers on the Hairline.

### Don't:
- **Don't** add shadows, gradients, motion or transitions (the legend's hatch swatch is drawn with a CSS gradient function, but it is a pattern, not a gradient).
- **Don't** color a reading by whether it is good or bad, and don't use red and green as a pair anywhere.
- **Don't** give the data line or any companion a party's hue (blue or red), or teal or green for companions.
- **Don't** use a dual y-axis, for a comparison or anything else.
- **Don't** let Show it as change the headline reading, the range strip or the sentence; say in a line over the chart what the chart shows instead.
- **Don't** reuse a companion color on one chart, or repaint a line when another is turned on or off.
- **Don't** add a second typeface or a brand color system beyond these tokens.
- **Don't** put watermarks across the plot area. **Don't** add text of the site's own to copied content beyond the title, reading, key, credit, address and license the image always carries. A reader's own words may go into the image they make, only in the labeled dashed band and as lettered markers (see The reader's words), never on the page or in a link.
